\noindent\textbf{Preview.}
A publicly known single driver gives an exact cutoff-WPBE and a closed-form
optimal menu.  With latent Poisson supply, the completion value of rescue
pricing is small in thin markets, largest at an intermediate thickness, and
small again in thick markets.  For
$(\alpha,\beta,\delta,\gamma)=(1,.8,.8,0)$, the deterministic numerical
solution peaks near $m=9.72$ and improves optimized completion by $6.16$
percentage points.  The endpoint pattern is proved; strict single-peakedness
is a conjecture.

\section{Exact one-rider/one-driver benchmark}

\subsection{Closed-form solution}

The rider has $v\sim U[0,1]$, the driver has $c\sim U[0,1]$, and the platform
maximizes completion.  Delayed rider value is $\beta v$ and delayed incumbent
surplus is $\delta(p_j-c)$.  The platform commits to
$0\le p_1\le p_2\le1$.  The number of drivers is publicly known to be exactly
one; there is no survival shock or fresh entry.

For an active menu $0\le p_1<p_2<\beta$, the driver accepts immediately iff
$c\le\ch$.  After rejection, the rider cancels, repeats $p_1$, or activates
$p_2$ according to
\[
v<\frac{p_1}{\beta},\qquad
\frac{p_1}{\beta}\le v\le v^M(\ch),\qquad
v>v^M(\ch),
\qquad
v^M(\ch)=\frac{p_1+p_2-\ch}{\beta}.
\]
The marginal driver's accept-versus-wait indifference gives the WPBE cutoff
\begin{equation}
\ch(p_1,p_2)=\left[p_1-
\frac{\delta(p_2-p_1)(\beta-p_2)}
{\beta(1-p_1)-\delta(\beta-p_2)}\right]_+ ,
\tag{1}
\end{equation}
and equilibrium completion is
\begin{equation}
M_1(p_1,p_2)=(1-p_1)\ch+
\frac{(\beta-p_2)(p_2-\ch)}{\beta}.
\tag{2}
\end{equation}
Thus the cutoff is calculated from the WPBE; the platform then optimizes over
menus subject to this equilibrium response.

Fixing a target cutoff reduces the two-price problem to one dimension.  The
unique continuation envelope and its implementing first price are
\begin{equation}
\widehat p_2(\ch)=\frac{\beta+\ch}{2},\qquad
\widehat p_1(\ch)=
\frac{1+\ch-\sqrt{\Delta(\ch)}}2,\qquad
\Delta(\ch)=(1-\ch)^2-\frac{\delta}{\beta}(\beta-\ch)^2.
\tag{3}
\end{equation}
Substitution into (2) gives
\begin{equation}
M_1^*=\max\left\{\frac14,\ \max_{\ch\in[0,\beta]}J_\delta(\ch)\right\},
\quad
J_\delta(\ch)=
\frac{\ch[1-\ch+\sqrt{\Delta(\ch)}]}2
+\frac{(\beta-\ch)^2}{4\beta}.
\tag{4}
\end{equation}
If $\beta\le1/2$ or $\delta=1$, the optimal value is the flat benchmark
$1/4$.  If $\beta>1/2$ and $\delta<1$, $J_\delta$ is strictly concave and has
a unique interior maximizer, which implements $p_1^*<p_2^*$.

\subsection{Equilibrium and the object reported later}

For continuous full-support $F$, every anonymous symmetric independent WPBE is
almost everywhere a pure cutoff: accept now iff $c\le a$.  The cutoff set
$\mathcal E_m(p_1,p_2)$ is nonempty and compact; thus the cutoff is a theorem,
not a numerical assumption.  Conditional on universal rejection, incumbents
are screened ($c>a$), entrants are unscreened, and
\[
\lambda_j(a)=m\!\left\{\alpha[F(p_j)-F(a)]+\gamma F(p_j)\right\},\quad
C_j(a)=1-e^{-\lambda_j(a)}.
\]
The rider compares $0$, $C_1(a)(\beta v-p_1)$, and
$C_2(a)(\beta v-p_2)$; the marginal driver's accept--wait indifference closes
the equilibrium.  In the general model the conservative comparison is
\[
\underline M_m(p_1,p_2)=\min_{a\in\mathcal E_m(p_1,p_2)}M_m(p_1,p_2;a),\quad
V(m)=\sup_{p_1\le p_2}\underline M_m(p_1,p_2)
      -\max_p\underline M_m(p,p).
\]
The baseline below sets $F=G=U[0,1]$ and $\gamma=0$, where every menu has a
unique cutoff, so the selection minimum is immaterial.  $100V(m)$ is the
percentage-point gain from one optimized policy over another at fixed $m$;
it is not the marginal value of adding drivers.

\subsection{No-entry baseline: what is held fixed and re-solved}

The baseline isolates strategic waiting:
\[
F=G=U[0,1],\qquad
(\alpha,\beta,\delta,\gamma)=(1,.8,.8,0).

The supporting theorem chain is
\[
\text{cutoff-WPBE}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{unique $a$}\ \Longrightarrow\

\widehat p_1^\delta(a)\ \Longrightarrow\

\subsection{Proposition and proof: the mixed-entry activity test}

Let $h(z)=(1-e^{-z})/z$ with $h(0)=1$, and define
\[
\mathcal A_{\alpha,\gamma}(p)
=m e^{-\gamma mp}(\alpha+\gamma)(\beta-p)
 -(1-e^{-\gamma mp}).
\]

\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1 (local rescue with fresh entry).}
Assume $F=G=U[0,1]$, $m>0$, $0<p<\beta<1$,
$0<\alpha<1$, $\gamma>0$, and $0<\delta\le1$.  If
$\mathcal A_{\alpha,\gamma}(p)>0$, then for every sufficiently small
$\varepsilon>0$ the menu $(p,p+\varepsilon)$ has a unique anonymous symmetric
cutoff equilibrium and
\[
a_\varepsilon=p-\kappa(p)\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon),\qquad
M_m^{\alpha,\gamma}(p,p+\varepsilon;a_\varepsilon)
=M_\gamma^{\rm flat}(m,p)+\Lambda(p)\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon),
\]
where $\kappa(p)>0$ and $\Lambda(p)>0$.  In particular, if an optimized flat
price $p^*$ satisfies $p^*<\beta$ and
$\mathcal A_{\alpha,\gamma}(p^*)>0$, a small committed rescue strictly
dominates optimized flat pricing.  If $\mathcal A_{\alpha,\gamma}(p)<0$, then
for every sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, the unique cutoff is $a=p$, no
rider uses rescue, and completion equals $M_\gamma^{\rm flat}(m,p)$ exactly.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Proof.}
Write $d=p-a$, $\rho=(1-p/\beta)/(1-p)$, and let $\eta_\varepsilon$ be the
posting-conditional rider mass choosing rescue.  Under
$(p,p+\varepsilon)$,
\[
\lambda_1=m(\alpha d+\gamma p),\qquad
\lambda_2=\lambda_1+m(\alpha+\gamma)\varepsilon. \tag{P1}
\]
Cutoff indifference is
\[
h(ma)d=\delta\alpha e^{-ma}
\{(\rho-\eta_\varepsilon)h(\lambda_1)d
+\eta_\varepsilon h(\lambda_2)(d+\varepsilon)\}. \tag{P2}
\]
Since $h(\lambda_j)\le1$, $\eta_\varepsilon\le\rho$, and
$h(ma)\ge e^{-ma}$, (P2) localizes every equilibrium:
\[
0\le d\le
\frac{\delta\alpha\rho}{1-\delta\alpha\rho}\varepsilon. \tag{P3}
\]
Set $x=mp$, $R=e^{-x}$, $E=e^{-\gamma x}$,
$\sigma=h(x)$, and $\ell=h(\gamma x)$.  On the compact rescaling
$y=d/\varepsilon$, the rider switch converges uniformly to
\[
\bar v=\frac1\beta\left[p+
\frac{1-E}{mE(\alpha+\gamma)}\right],\qquad
\mathcal A_{\alpha,\gamma}(p)>0\iff\bar v<1.
\]
Hence $\eta_\varepsilon\to\eta^0=(1-\bar v)/(1-p)>0$, and (P2) divided by
$\varepsilon$ converges in $C^1$ to
\[
[\sigma-\delta R\alpha\rho\ell]y
-\delta R\alpha\eta^0\ell=0. \tag{P4}
\]
Its slope is positive because
$\sigma-\delta R\alpha\rho\ell\ge\sigma-R>0$.  Thus the nearby cutoff is
unique and
\[
\kappa(p)=\frac{\delta R\alpha\eta^0\ell}
{\sigma-\delta R\alpha\rho\ell}>0.
\]
Differentiating completion along this branch gives
\[
\Lambda(p)=m(1-p)R\eta^0
\frac{T_\delta}{\sigma-\delta R\alpha\rho\ell}>0,
\quad
T_\delta=E(\alpha+\gamma)\sigma-
\delta R\alpha\ell\{1-\rho+\rho E(1+\gamma)\}. \tag{P5}
\]
The sign lemma in the current proof establishes
$\mathcal A>0\Rightarrow T_\delta>0$: for $\delta=1$ it follows by splitting
on $1-E(1+\gamma)$ and applying convexity of the exponential; lowering
$\delta$ adds a nonnegative term.  If $\mathcal A<0$, then $\bar v>1$ and
uniform convergence makes $\eta_\varepsilon=0$ throughout the localized set.
Equation (P2) then leaves $a=p$ as the unique cutoff and the rescue option is
unused. \hfill$\square$

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Scope.}  This is a local theorem around an active price, not a
global solution of the mixed-entry menu problem and not a monotonicity theorem
in $\gamma$.

The main numerical curve is backed by the exact chain on p.~4.  This page then

\noindent\textbf{Bottom line.}
The closest synthesis is Correa's commitment and waiting, Li--Kuo's latent
Poisson competition, Lee--Li's continuation composition, and Hu--Hu--Zhu's
two-sided temporal response.  The incremental contribution is their joint
cutoff-WPBE design under public failure and the optimized completion comparison
against flat pricing.

\paragraph{Three conclusions for the meeting.}
Rescue is valuable at intermediate latent thickness; its value is recovery
after failure net of announcement-induced waiting; and composition cannot be
reduced to continuation headcount.  Profit and rider-payment overlays would
broaden the managerial claim without changing this completion theorem chain.
