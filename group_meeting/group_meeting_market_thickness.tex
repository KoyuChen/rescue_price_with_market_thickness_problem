\noindent\textbf{Main result.}
Write $V(m)$ for the optimized completion gain of the best rescue menu over the
best flat price at public thickness $m$.  We first solve the cutoff-WPBE and
optimal menu for a publicly known single driver, then embed the same mechanism
in latent Poisson supply.  In the uniform no-entry model with $\beta\ge1/2$,
$V(m)>0$ at every finite thickness and converges to zero at both thickness
endpoints.  When $\alpha=1$ and $\gamma=0$, the exact-one-driver gain is the
coefficient of $m$ in the thin-market expansion of $V(m)$.

\section{Exact one-rider/one-driver benchmark and optimal menu}

\subsection{Closed-form solution}

Here the number of incumbents is publicly known to be exactly one.  The rider has
$v\sim U[0,1]$, the driver has $c\sim U[0,1]$, and the platform maximizes the probability
of completion.  The rider's delayed value is $\beta v$; the incumbent's delayed surplus is
$\delta(p_j-c)$.  The platform commits to $0\le p_1\le p_2\le1$, with
$\beta,\delta\in(0,1]$; $j\in\{1,2\}$ indexes the active payment.  There is no
fresh entry in this benchmark.

For an interior active menu $0\le p_1<p_2<\beta$, consider an anonymous
pure-cutoff WPBE in which the driver accepts now iff $c\le\ch$ and both
continuation actions occur for positive rider masses.  In equilibrium the rider
posts exactly when $v\ge p_1$.  Bayes' rule gives
$v\mid\text{post}\sim U[p_1,1]$ and, following refusal,
$c\mid\text{refusal}\sim U[\ch,1]$.  Following refusal, the probability that
the driver accepts $p_j$ is $(p_j-\ch)/(1-\ch)$.  Equating the rider's expected
payoffs from repeating $p_1$ and activating $p_2$ gives the switching value
$v^M(\ch)$ and the regions
\[
\begin{array}{ccl}
v<p_1/\beta &:& \text{cancel},\\
p_1/\beta\le v\le v^M(\ch) &:& \text{repeat }p_1,\\
v>v^M(\ch) &:& \text{activate rescue }p_2,
\end{array}
\qquad
v^M(\ch)=\frac{p_1+p_2-\ch}{\beta}.
\]
Conditional on a posted request, the rider masses who repeat and rescue are
$(p_2-\ch)/[\beta(1-p_1)]$ and
$(\beta-p_1-p_2+\ch)/[\beta(1-p_1)]$.  Hence the cutoff driver's discounted
wait payoff is
\[
\begin{aligned}
W_1(\ch)
&=\frac{\delta}{\beta(1-p_1)}
\{(p_2-\ch)(p_1-\ch)
+(\beta-p_1-p_2+\ch)(p_2-\ch)\}\\
&=\frac{\delta(\beta-p_2)(p_2-\ch)}{\beta(1-p_1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Equating $W_1(\ch)$ to the immediate payoff $p_1-\ch$ and solving for the
cutoff gives
\begin{equation}
\ch(p_1,p_2)=\left[p_1-
\frac{\delta(p_2-p_1)(\beta-p_2)}
{\beta(1-p_1)-\delta(\beta-p_2)}\right]_+ .
\tag{1}
\end{equation}
where $[x]_+=\max\{x,0\}$ implements the reject-all boundary.
At $p_2=p_1$, $p_2\ge\beta$, or $p_1\ge\beta$, the rescue branch is inactive and
the cutoff is $\ch=p_1$.

Immediate completion has probability $(1-p_1)\ch$.  The unconditional repeat
and rescue completion probabilities are, respectively,
\[
M_{\mathrm{rep}}=\frac{(p_2-\ch)(p_1-\ch)}{\beta},\qquad
M_{\mathrm{res}}=
\frac{(\beta-p_1-p_2+\ch)(p_2-\ch)}{\beta}.
\]
Their sum collapses to the second term below, so total completion is
\begin{equation}
M_1(p_1,p_2)=(1-p_1)\ch+
\frac{(\beta-p_2)(p_2-\ch)}{\beta}.
\tag{2}
\end{equation}
If $\ch>0$, the same marginal-driver indifference can be written as
\begin{equation}
(1-p_1)(p_1-\ch)=\frac{\delta}{\beta}(\beta-p_2)(p_2-\ch),
\tag{3}
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
M_1=(1-p_1)\left[\ch+\frac{p_1-\ch}{\delta}\right]
=p_1(1-p_1)+\frac{(1-p_1)(1-\delta)}{\delta}(p_1-\ch).
\tag{4}
\end{equation}
Here $p_1(1-p_1)$ is completion under a flat menu at the same first price; the
last term is therefore the within-$p_1$ gain created by rescue.
For $0<\ch<p_1$, a rescue menu strictly improves completion relative to the same
$p_1$ when $\delta<1$.  Every positive-cutoff active menu is neutral relative
to the same $p_1$ when $\delta=1$.

\paragraph{One-dimensional reduction.}
Fix a target cutoff $\ch$.  The continuation component depending on $p_2$ is
$H(p_2;\ch)=(\beta-p_2)(p_2-\ch)/\beta$, with
\[
H_{p_2}=\frac{\beta+\ch-2p_2}{\beta},\qquad
H_{p_2p_2}=-\frac{2}{\beta}<0.
\]
Thus its unique envelope maximizer is
\[
\widehat p_2(\ch)=\frac{\beta+\ch}{2}.
\]
At this rescue price, (3) becomes
\[
(1-p_1)(p_1-\ch)=\frac{\delta(\beta-\ch)^2}{4\beta}.
\]
The two quadratic roots are
$[1+\ch\pm\sqrt{\Delta(\ch)}]/2$.  The plus root exceeds
$(1+\ch)/2\ge\widehat p_2(\ch)$ and violates $p_1\le p_2$; hence the feasible
root is
\begin{equation}
\Delta(\ch)=(1-\ch)^2-\frac{\delta}{\beta}(\beta-\ch)^2,
\qquad
\widehat p_1(\ch)=\frac{1+\ch-\sqrt{\Delta(\ch)}}{2}.
\tag{5}
\end{equation}
The best flat menu solves $\max_{p_1\in[0,1]}p_1(1-p_1)=1/4$.
Substituting the two envelope prices into (2) gives $J_\delta(\ch)$, so the
platform compares the best active menu with this flat benchmark:
\begin{equation}
M_1^*=\max\left\{\frac14,\ \max_{\ch\in[0,\beta]}J_\delta(\ch)\right\},
\qquad
J_\delta(\ch)=
\frac{\ch[1-\ch+\sqrt{\Delta(\ch)}]}{2}
+\frac{(\beta-\ch)^2}{4\beta}.
\tag{6}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Optimal menu.}
If $\beta\le1/2$, a flat menu $p_1=p_2=1/2$ is optimal and $M_1^*=1/4$.
If $\delta=1$, the same value obtains for every $\beta$.  If $\beta>1/2$ and
$\delta<1$, $J_\delta$ is strictly concave and has a unique interior maximizer
$\ch^*\in(0,\beta)$; the optimal rescue menu is
$p_1^*=\widehat p_1(\ch^*)<p_2^*=\widehat p_2(\ch^*)$.

For $\beta>1/2$ and $\delta<1$, the unique interior cutoff solves
\begin{equation}
1-\delta\beta+3(\delta-1)\ch+2(1-\delta/\beta)\ch^2
=\frac{2\beta-1}{\beta}\ch\sqrt{\Delta(\ch)}.
\tag{7}
\end{equation}
Indeed,
\[
\Delta'(\ch)=2(\delta-1)+2(1-\delta/\beta)\ch,
\quad
J_\delta'(\ch)=\frac12\left[
\sqrt{\Delta}+\ch(1/\beta-2)
+\frac{\ch\Delta'}{2\sqrt{\Delta}}\right],
\]
and clearing the positive denominator in $J_\delta'(\ch)=0$ gives (7).

\subsection{Numerical illustration}

For the calibration used below, $\beta=\delta=4/5$.  Equation~(7) becomes
\[
12-20\ch=5\ch\sqrt{9-10\ch}.
\]
Squaring gives
$(5\ch-4)(50\ch^2+75\ch-36)=0$.  The root $\ch=4/5$
violates the unsquared equation, while the negative quadratic root is
infeasible.  The remaining quadratic has discriminant
$75^2+4(50)(36)=225\cdot57$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
\ch^*=\frac{3(\sqrt{57}-5)}{20},\qquad
p_1^*=\frac{11+\sqrt{57}}{40},\qquad
p_2^*=\frac{1+3\sqrt{57}}{40},
\tag{8}
\end{equation}
Substituting $\ch^*$ into (5) gives the two prices; substituting the resulting
menu into (2) gives
\[
M_1^*=\frac{1261-57\sqrt{57}}{3200}=25.9581\%,
\qquad 100\left(M_1^*-\frac14\right)=0.9581\text{ percentage points}.
\]
Unconditional completion decomposes into $20.5104\%$ in the first window,
$2.1208\%$ after repeating $p_1$, and $3.3269\%$ after activating $p_2$.
These components sum to $M_1^*=25.9581\%$.  The menu and decomposition depend
on $(\beta,\delta)$.

\section{Formal market-thickness setup}

Market thickness is public; the realized supply count is latent and individual
types are private.  The platform commits to $0\le p_1\le p_2\le1$.  The
primitives are:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{1.7cm}L{2.4cm}X@{}}
\toprule
Primitive & Domain & Definition \\
\midrule
$m$ & $(0,\infty)$ & Public mean of incumbent supply; the latent count is
$N^I\sim\operatorname{Pois}(m)$.\\
$\alpha$ & $[0,1]$ & Probability that a waiting incumbent remains available in
the terminal window.\\
$\gamma$ & $[0,\infty)$ & Fresh-entry intensity relative to $m$; after failure,
$N^E\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\gamma m)$.\\
$\beta$ & $(0,1]$ & Rider discount factor; delayed gross value is $\beta v$.\\
$\delta$ & $(0,1]$ & Incumbent discount factor; delayed surplus at $p_j$ is
$\delta(p_j-c_i)$.\\
$F$ & continuous CDF on $[0,1]$ & Distribution of driver costs $c_i\sim F$;
draws are i.i.d. and an incumbent's cost persists across windows.\\
$G$ & continuous CDF on $[0,1]$ & Distribution of the rider's private value
$v\sim G$.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
All primitives and the menu are common knowledge; realizations are observed only
as specified below.  The numerical analysis sets $F=G=U[0,1]$ and $\gamma=0$.
In $V(m)$, only $m$ varies; $(\alpha,\gamma,\beta,\delta,F,G)$ are held fixed.
Throughout, $j\in\{1,2\}$ indexes the payment used in the terminal window.

\subsection{Timing and information}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Stage \arabic*.},leftmargin=2.0cm,
                  itemsep=3pt,topsep=2pt]

\item \textbf{Commitment.}
The platform observes $m>0$ and commits publicly to
$0\le p_1\le p_2\le1$.  It cannot revise either payment after subsequent
actions, and $p_2$ can be activated only after first-round universal rejection.

\item \textbf{Rider entry.}
The rider privately observes $v\sim G$, observes $(m,p_1,p_2)$, and chooses
whether to post.  Leaving gives zero; posting itself generates no transfer.

\item \textbf{Latent incumbent supply.}
Conditional on a posted request,
\[
N^I\sim\operatorname{Pois}(m).
\]
The realization is latent to the platform, rider, and drivers.  Each incumbent
privately observes only her persistent cost $c_i\sim F$ and simultaneously
chooses whether to accept $p_1$ or wait.  She does not observe the number,
costs, or actions of rival drivers when deciding.

\item \textbf{Initial assignment or public failure.}
If at least one incumbent accepts, one acceptor is selected uniformly and the
request completes with selected-pair payoff $(v-p_1,p_1-c_i)$; unselected
acceptors receive zero.  If none accepts, public failure reveals only universal
rejection, not the realized count or costs.

\item \textbf{Rider continuation.}
After failure, the rider chooses cancel, repeat $p_1$, or activate $p_2$ using
$v$, the committed menu, and the failure posterior.

\item \textbf{Survival, entry, and terminal assignment.}
Following continuation, each waiting incumbent remains available independently
with probability $\alpha$.  Independently, fresh supply
$N^E\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\gamma m)$ arrives and draws i.i.d. costs from $F$.
Counts, survival realizations, and rival costs remain latent.  At the active
payment $p_j$, available drivers make their terminal accept decisions.  If at
least one accepts, one is selected uniformly.  The rider receives
$\beta v-p_j$; a selected incumbent receives $\delta(p_j-c_i)$, while a selected
fresh entrant receives $p_j-c_i$.  If nobody accepts, the request expires and
all players receive zero.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Benchmark distinction.}
Unlike the benchmark above, $m=1$ denotes a hidden
$N^I\sim\operatorname{Pois}(1)$ count, not a publicly known single driver.

\paragraph{Equilibrium class.}
We study anonymous symmetric pure-cutoff WPBE.  An incumbent accepts $p_1$
exactly when $c_i$ lies below a cutoff $\ch$.  Beliefs follow Bayes' rule after
posting and universal rejection.  Poisson splitting determines continuation
supply after failure, while Palm conditioning leaves a focal incumbent's
number of rivals Poisson with mean $m$.  Thus $\ch$ is derived from
accept-versus-wait indifference, not selected from an observed driver count.
The formal setup allows $\gamma\ge0$; the global optimization and numerical
evidence below specialize to $\gamma=0$.

\subsection*{WPBE equations and completion}

For a candidate accept-now cutoff $\ch$, define
\[
\phi(x)=\frac{1-e^{-x}}{x},\quad \phi(0)=1,\qquad
\lambda_j(\ch)=m\{\alpha[F(p_j)-F(\ch)]+\gamma F(p_j)\},\qquad
C_j(\ch)=1-e^{-\lambda_j(\ch)}.
\]
For $K\sim\operatorname{Pois}(x)$,
\[
\Pr(K=0)=e^{-x},\qquad
\mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{1+K}\right]
=\frac{1-e^{-x}}x=\phi(x).
\]
Thus $\phi(x)$ is a focal acceptor's expected assignment share when her rivals
have Poisson mean $x$.  Poisson thinning gives $\lambda_j(\ch)$ as the mean
terminal supply eligible at $p_j$, and $C_j(\ch)$ is the probability that this
eligible set is nonempty.
After universal rejection the rider compares
\[
0,\qquad C_1(\ch)(\beta v-p_1),\qquad C_2(\ch)(\beta v-p_2).
\]
If $C_2(\ch)>C_1(\ch)$, the repeat/rescue boundary is
\begin{equation}
v^M(\ch)=
\frac{p_2C_2(\ch)-p_1C_1(\ch)}
{\beta[C_2(\ch)-C_1(\ch)]}.
\tag{9}
\end{equation}
Equation (9) comes from equating the last two payoffs.  If
$C_2(\ch)=C_1(\ch)$, rescue adds no coverage and we set $v^M(\ch)=+\infty$.
Conditional on posting, define
\[
\eta^{\mathrm{rep}}(\ch)
=\Pr\{p_1/\beta\le v\le v^M(\ch)\mid v\ge p_1\},\qquad
\eta^{\mathrm{res}}(\ch)
=\Pr\{v>v^M(\ch)\mid v\ge p_1\},
\]
with empty regions assigned zero mass.  A focal incumbent of type $c$ compares
\begin{align}
A(c;\ch)&=\phi(mF(\ch))(p_1-c),\nonumber\\
W(c;\ch)&=\delta\alpha e^{-mF(\ch)}
\{\eta^{\mathrm{rep}}(\ch)\phi(\lambda_1(\ch))(p_1-c)^+
+\eta^{\mathrm{res}}(\ch)\phi(\lambda_2(\ch))(p_2-c)^+\}.
\tag{10}
\end{align}
Here $x^+=\max\{x,0\}$.  Both payoffs are conditional on a posted request.
In $A$, $\phi(mF(\ch))$ is the focal driver's initial assignment share.  In
$W$, $e^{-mF(\ch)}$ is the probability that all rivals reject initially,
$\alpha$ is the focal incumbent's survival probability, and
$\phi(\lambda_j)$ is her terminal assignment share.
An interior cutoff satisfies $A(\ch;\ch)=W(\ch;\ch)$; one-sided inequalities
apply at $0$ and $p_1$.  Completion is
\begin{equation}
M(p_1,p_2;\ch)=(1-G(p_1))\left[1-e^{-mF(\ch)}+e^{-mF(\ch)}
\{\eta^{\mathrm{rep}}(\ch)C_1(\ch)
+\eta^{\mathrm{res}}(\ch)C_2(\ch)\}\right].
\tag{11}
\end{equation}
The factor $1-G(p_1)$ is the posting probability.  Inside the brackets, the
first term is initial coverage and the second is failure followed by terminal
recovery.

\paragraph{Selection.}
In the uniform no-entry model, every menu has a unique \emph{equilibrium cutoff value}
within this WPBE class; null-type actions and payoff-irrelevant beliefs may differ.

\section{Solving $p_1^*(m),p_2^*(m)$ from the WPBE}

Set $F=G=U[0,1]$, $\gamma=0$, $\alpha\in(0,1]$, and $m>0$.  Cutoff
indifference yields the following scalar solution.

\paragraph{Step 1: solve the rescue-price envelope at a fixed cutoff.}
Let $k=\alpha m$, the effective mean surviving-incumbent supply per unit cost
mass.  Under uniform types and no entry,
$C_j=1-e^{-k(p_j-\ch)}$.  Substituting the switching value (9) makes repeat and
rescue completion telescope:
\[
C_1\left(v^M-\frac{p_1}{\beta}\right)+C_2(1-v^M)
=C_2\left(1-\frac{p_2}{\beta}\right).
\]
Therefore the continuation term, before multiplication by the first-round
failure probability $e^{-m\ch}$, is
\[
\frac{S_m(\ch,p_2)}{\beta},\qquad
S_m(\ch,p_2)=(\beta-p_2)\{1-e^{-k(p_2-\ch)}\}.
\]
For fixed $\ch$, this expression is strictly concave in $p_2\in[\ch,\beta]$.
Indeed,
\[
\frac{\partial S_m}{\partial p_2}
=-(1-e^{-k(p_2-\ch)})
+k(\beta-p_2)e^{-k(p_2-\ch)},\qquad
\frac{\partial^2 S_m}{\partial p_2^2}
=-e^{-k(p_2-\ch)}\{2k+k^2(\beta-p_2)\}<0.
\]
The first-order condition for its unique maximizer
$\widehat p_2(\ch;m)$ is
\begin{equation}
e^{k(\widehat p_2-\ch)}-1=k(\beta-\widehat p_2).
\tag{12}
\end{equation}
Let $z=k(\beta-\widehat p_2)$ and $y=1+z$.  Equation (12) gives
$y e^y=e^{1+k(\beta-\ch)}$, so
\begin{equation}
\widehat p_2(\ch;m)=
\beta-\frac{W_0\!\left(e^{1+k(\beta-\ch)}\right)-1}{k}.
\tag{13}
\end{equation}
Here $W_0$ is the principal Lambert-$W$ function, defined by
$W_0(x)e^{W_0(x)}=x$.  Its argument is positive, and $W_0$ is the unique real
branch that delivers $z\ge0$ and $\ch<\widehat p_2<\beta$ for an interior
cutoff.  Since $e^x-1>x$ for $x>0$, (12) also implies
$\widehat p_2<(\beta+\ch)/2$.
Write $S_m(\ch)=S_m(\ch,\widehat p_2(\ch;m))$.

\paragraph{Step 2: impose the driver's WPBE indifference.}
Define
\begin{equation}
h_m(\ch)=\frac{e^{m\ch}-1}{\ch},\qquad h_m(0)=m.
\tag{14}
\end{equation}
The value at zero follows from continuity.  The function $h_m$ rewrites the
cutoff indifference in terms of the odds of reaching the failure node.
The direct cutoff equation is
\begin{equation}
(p_1-\ch)h_m(\ch)=
\frac{\delta S_m(\ch)}{\beta(1-p_1)}.
\tag{15}
\end{equation}
Multiplying by $1-p_1$ and writing
$R_m(\ch)=\delta S_m(\ch)/[\beta h_m(\ch)]$ gives the quadratic
\[
(1-p_1)(p_1-\ch)=R_m(\ch),\qquad
p_1^2-(1+\ch)p_1+\ch+R_m(\ch)=0.
\]
Its roots are
$[1+\ch\pm\sqrt{(1-\ch)^2-4R_m(\ch)}]/2$.  The plus root exceeds
$(1+\ch)/2>\widehat p_2$ and violates $p_1\le p_2$; the implementing root is
\begin{equation}
\widehat p_1^\delta(\ch;m)=
\frac{1+\ch-
\sqrt{(1-\ch)^2-\dfrac{4\delta S_m(\ch)}{\beta h_m(\ch)}}}{2}.
\tag{16}
\end{equation}
The nonnegative discriminant is the feasibility condition for implementing
$\ch$, and the selected branch satisfies
$\ch\le\widehat p_1^\delta<\widehat p_2$.  Equation (15) links the rescue price
to initial coverage through the driver's waiting incentive.

\paragraph{Step 3: optimize the single remaining state variable.}
Substitute (13) and (16) into completion:
\begin{equation}
J_{\delta,m}(\ch)=
[1-\widehat p_1^\delta(\ch;m)](1-e^{-m\ch})
+e^{-m\ch}\frac{S_m(\ch)}{\beta}.
\tag{17}
\end{equation}
The first term is initial completion: posting probability times initial
coverage.  The second is the telescoped continuation term above, multiplied by
the probability of initial failure.
For $\beta\ge1/2$, the optimal rescue-menu solution is characterized by
\begin{equation}
\ch^*(m)\in\arg\max_{\ch\in[0,\beta]}J_{\delta,m}(\ch),\qquad
p_1^*(m)=\widehat p_1^\delta(\ch^*(m);m),\quad
p_2^*(m)=\widehat p_2(\ch^*(m);m).
\tag{18}
\end{equation}
For $\beta\ge1/2$, the no-entry global reduction places every optimizer on the
strict envelope just derived, so (18) is equivalent to the original two-price
problem for every public $m$.  The cutoff is
unique within the anonymous symmetric pure-cutoff WPBE class, but the outer
argmax need not be.  If $\beta<1/2$, (18) must also be compared with the
flat/repeat-only branch $p_1\ge\beta$.

\section{Equilibrium regimes and a reproducible numerical certificate}

\paragraph{Rescue value at a fixed thickness.}
In the uniform no-entry model, let $M_m(p_1,p_2)$ denote the unconditional
completion probability in (11), evaluated at the menu's unique equilibrium
cutoff value within the maintained cutoff-WPBE class.  Holding all other
primitives fixed,
define
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_R^*(m)
  &=\max_{0\le p_1\le p_2\le1}M_m(p_1,p_2),\\
M_F^*(m)
  &=\max_{p_1\in[0,1]}M_m(p_1,p_1),\\
V(m)&=M_R^*(m)-M_F^*(m).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $R$ denotes the best committed rescue menu and $F$ the best single-price
policy.  Thus $V(m)$ is the increase in optimized equilibrium completion made
possible by the rescue option at the same public thickness $m$; $100V(m)$ is
that increase in percentage points.  It is not the value of increasing $m$.
Since single-price policies are included among rescue menus, $V(m)\ge0$ by
construction.

For a fixed first price, let
\[
p_z(m)=\widehat p_1^\delta(0;m)
=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\delta S_m(0)/(\beta m)}}2.
\]
Thus $p_z(m)$ is the largest first price for which the optimized continuation
can be implemented at the reject-all boundary $\ch=0$.
The optimal continuation has three regimes.

\toprule

\midrule
$0\le p_1\le p_z(m)$ & reject-all boundary $\ch=0$ &
$p_2=\widehat p_2(0;m)$ \\
$p_z(m)<p_1<\beta$ & unique $\ch\in(0,p_1)$ satisfying
$(p_1-\ch)h_m(\ch)=\delta S_m(\ch)/[\beta(1-p_1)]$ &
$p_2=\widehat p_2(\ch;m)$ \\
$\beta\le p_1\le1$ & rescue unused & all $p_2\ge p_1$ are outcome-equivalent \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\subsection*{Exact benchmark as the thin-market limit}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{3.0cm}XX@{}}
\toprule

\midrule

The exact benchmark is the thin-market limit of the latent-thickness model.
With $\alpha=1$, $\gamma=0$, and $m\downarrow0$,
uniformly over the compact feasible price and cutoff sets,
\[
1-e^{-m(p_2-\ch)}=m(p_2-\ch)+O(m^2),\qquad
h_m(\ch)=m+O(m^2).
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{S_m(\ch,p_2)}{m}\longrightarrow
(\beta-p_2)(p_2-\ch),\qquad
\frac{h_m(\ch)}{m}\longrightarrow1.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\widehat p_2(\ch;m)\longrightarrow\frac{\beta+\ch}{2},\qquad
\widehat p_1^\delta(\ch;m)\longrightarrow\widehat p_1(\ch),\qquad
\frac{J_{\delta,m}(\ch)}{m}\longrightarrow J_\delta(\ch).
\]
The flat objective divided by $m$ converges uniformly to $p_1(1-p_1)$.
Uniform convergence and the maximum theorem permit the maximization to pass
through the limit.  Equivalently, a Poisson market contains exactly one
incumbent with probability $m+o(m)$ and at least two with probability $o(m)$.
For $\beta=\delta=.8$, this nesting gives
\[
\lim_{m\downarrow0}\frac{V(m)}{m}
=M_1^*-\frac14
=\frac{461-57\sqrt{57}}{3200}=0.00958107.
\]
The numerator follows directly from
$(1261-57\sqrt{57})-800=461-57\sqrt{57}$ when the flat value $1/4$ is
subtracted from $M_1^*$.
Thus the one-driver improvement is exactly the first-order coefficient of the
rescue value near an empty market.

\paragraph{Contribution from latent thickness.}
Latent thickness adds assignment competition in an unobserved driver pool and
Bayesian updating after universal rejection.  Their interaction with menu
commitment and discounted waiting yields the scalar cutoff-WPBE design problem
and the value function $V(m)$.

\paragraph{First-order certificate for the outer problem.}
At an interior solution, put $R_m=\delta S_m/(\beta h_m)$ and suppress
$(\ch;m)$ arguments.  Primes denote total derivatives with respect to $\ch$,
holding $m$ and all other primitives fixed.  The envelope theorem for $S_m$,
direct differentiation of $h_m$, and implicit differentiation of
$(1-p_1)(p_1-\ch)=R_m$ give
\begin{align}
\frac{S_m'}{S_m}&=-\frac{1}{\beta-\widehat p_2},
&\frac{h_m'}{h_m}&=\frac{m}{1-e^{-m\ch}}-\frac1\ch,\nonumber\\
R_m'&=R_m\left(\frac{S_m'}{S_m}-\frac{h_m'}{h_m}\right),
&(\widehat p_1^\delta)'&=
\frac{1-\widehat p_1^\delta+R_m'}{1+\ch-2\widehat p_1^\delta}.
\tag{19}
\end{align}
The scalar first-order condition is
\begin{equation}
J_{\delta,m}'(\ch)=
-(1-e^{-m\ch})(\widehat p_1^\delta)'
+me^{-m\ch}(1-\widehat p_1^\delta)
+\frac{e^{-m\ch}}{\beta}(S_m'-mS_m)=0.
\tag{20}
\end{equation}
The numerical procedure solves (12) and (15), brackets and enumerates every root
of the scalar condition (20) on $[0,\beta]$, checks the relevant second-order
signs, and compares all stationary and boundary values.  Rider best responses
are then recovered from (9), and driver deviations are checked on both sides
of the cutoff.  This gives a global one-dimensional WPBE certificate.

\paragraph{Reduced forms.}
For a flat price $p_1=p_2$, the equilibrium cutoff is $\ch=p_1$ and the
continuation branch is unused.  Hence
$f_m(p_1)=(1-p_1)(1-e^{-mp_1})$, with
\[
f_m'(p_1)=-(1-e^{-mp_1})+m(1-p_1)e^{-mp_1},\qquad
f_m''(p_1)=-e^{-mp_1}\{2m+m^2(1-p_1)\}<0.
\]
The unique first-order condition is $e^{mp_1}-1=m(1-p_1)$.  Setting
$y=1+m-mp_1$ gives $ye^y=e^{1+m}$ and therefore
\begin{equation}
M_F^*(m)=\max_{p_1\in[0,1]}(1-p_1)(1-e^{-mp_1}),
\qquad
p_F^*(m)=\frac{1+m-W_0(e^{1+m})}{m}<\frac12.
\tag{21}
\end{equation}
Since $e^{m/2}-1>m/2$, $f_m'(1/2)<0$ and the unique flat optimum lies below
$1/2$.
For $\beta\ge1/2$, the rescue-menu optimum reduces to
\begin{equation}
M_R^*(m)=\max_{\ch\in[0,\beta]}J_{\delta,m}(\ch).
\tag{22}
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Strict finite-thickness gain.}
Take $p_1=p_F^*(m)<1/2\le\beta$ and a sufficiently small strict increment
$p_1<p_2<\beta$.  Its equilibrium has $0<\ch<p_1$, and (15)--(17) imply
\[
M_m(p_1,p_2)
=(1-p_1)m\phi(m\ch)
\left[\ch+\frac{p_1-\ch}{\delta}\right].
\]
The bracket is at least $p_1$ because $\delta\le1$, while $\phi$ is strictly
decreasing and $\ch<p_1$.  Thus
\[
M_m(p_1,p_2)>
(1-p_1)m\phi(mp_1)p_1
=(1-p_1)(1-e^{-mp_1})=M_F^*(m).
\]
This same-first-price comparison proves $V(m)>0$ at every finite $m$.

\paragraph{Interior-thickness mechanism.}
When $m$ is small, there is rarely a driver to rescue.  As $m$ grows, universal
rejection still occurs but the failed request is increasingly likely to face a useful
backup set, so a contingent $p_2$ creates option value.  When $m$ is very large, the
optimized flat price already completes almost every request; the remaining improvement
must again shrink.

\subsection*{Analytical properties of $V(m)$}

Under $F=G=U[0,1]$, $\gamma=0$, $\alpha\in(0,1]$, $\beta\ge1/2$, and
$\delta\in(0,1]$, the following properties hold for every finite $m>0$:
\[
V(m)>0,\qquad V\text{ is continuous},\qquad
\lim_{m\downarrow0}V(m)=\lim_{m\to\infty}V(m)=0.
\]
Hence there is some $m^*\in(0,\infty)$ such that
\[
V(m^*)=\max_{m>0}V(m)>0.
\]
This guarantees a finite interior maximizer, but not uniqueness or
single-peakedness.

\paragraph{Endpoint argument.}
On every compact interval of positive $m$, the continuous extensions at
$\ch=0$ make $J_{\delta,m}(\ch)$ jointly continuous in $(m,\ch)$.  The maximum
theorem applied to (21)--(22) makes both optimized values, and hence $V$,
continuous; optimizer uniqueness is unnecessary.

As $m\downarrow0$, any completion requires at least one incumbent, so
\[
0\le V(m)\le M_R^*(m)\le1-e^{-m}\le m\longrightarrow0.
\]
As $m\to\infty$, the feasible flat price $p_1=(\log m)/m$ completes with
probability $(1-\log(m)/m)(1-1/m)\to1$.  Therefore
$0\le V(m)\le1-M_F^*(m)\to0$.  Positivity at any fixed $m_0$, these endpoint
bounds, continuity, and the Weierstrass theorem then place at least one global
maximizer in a finite interval inside $(0,\infty)$; they do not imply
uniqueness or single-peakedness.

\paragraph{Tail rates.}
For the plotted calibration, the thin-market calculation above gives
\[
V(m)\sim \kappa m,\qquad
\kappa=\frac{461-57\sqrt{57}}{3200}=0.00958107,
\qquad (m\downarrow0).
\]
For the thick-market rate, put $x=m\ch$ and $T=S_m/\beta$.  Equation (17) has
the exact loss decomposition
\[
1-J_{\delta,m}=p_1(1-e^{-x})+(1-T)e^{-x}.
\]
Using (12)--(16), the feasible choice $x=\log\log m$ yields
$p_1=(1+o(1))x/m$ and $(1-T)e^{-x}=O(1/m)$.  Conversely, for
$p_2\le\beta/2$ every feasible policy satisfies
\[
m(1-J_{\delta,m})
\ge x(1-e^{-x})+C_m e^{-x},\qquad
C_m=\frac{\log(1+\alpha m\beta/2)}{\alpha\beta},
\]
whose minimum is asymptotic to $\log C_m\sim\log\log m$; policies with
$p_2>\beta/2$ have a larger first loss term.  Hence
\[
1-M_R^*(m)\sim\frac{\log\log m}{m}.
\]
For the flat optimizer, let $x_F=mp_F^*$.  Its first-order condition gives
$e^{x_F}=1+m-x_F$, so
\[
1-M_F^*(m)=\frac{x_F+1-e^{-x_F}}m
\sim\frac{\log m}{m}.
\]
Thus, for fixed $\alpha,\beta>0$, $\delta\in(0,1]$, and $\gamma=0$,
$V(m)\sim(\log m)/m$.  The displayed single-hump shape below remains
numerical evidence rather than an analytical shape result.

\subsection*{Type regions across market thickness}
For each row in the table, let
\[
C_j^*(m)=1-e^{-m[p_j^*(m)-\ch^*(m)]},\qquad
v^{M*}(m)=
\frac{p_2^*(m)C_2^*(m)-p_1^*(m)C_1^*(m)}
{\beta[C_2^*(m)-C_1^*(m)]}.
\]
The rider widths are
$(p_1^*,\ p_1^*/\beta-p_1^*,\ v^{M*}-p_1^*/\beta,\ 1-v^{M*})$;
the driver widths are
$(\ch^*,\ p_1^*-\ch^*,\ p_2^*-p_1^*,\ 1-p_2^*)$.
They have the same four meanings as in Figure~\ref{fig:type-regions}, but both
the optimal menu and the rider's coverage-adjusted switching value are
recomputed at every $m$.
