\section{Three supply regimes isolate arrivals before search}

\takeaway{Pages 1--2 solve the closed, known-one-driver benchmark.  With latent
Poisson supply, we first ask what a new period-2 cohort changes at the same
fixed footprint; only then do we let the platform expand notification reach.}

\subsection{Common Poisson environment}

The two response windows have equal length and the core footprint has area one.
The first-window incumbents and second-window core arrivals are
\[
N_1\sim\operatorname{Pois}(m),\qquad
N_2^C\sim\operatorname{Pois}(m),\qquad N_1\perp N_2^C.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus $m$ is a \emph{per-window cohort flow}, not a stock counted twice.
Every incumbent in $N_1$ sees the focal order and the full commitment before
choosing accept or reject-and-wait.  Fresh drivers in $N_2^C$ first see it only
if a terminal notification is sent.  A rejector remains physically available
for the terminal lottery with exogenous probability $\omega\in[0,1]$.

Throughout this numerical framework, rider value $v$ and persistent driver
cost $c$ satisfy $v,c\sim U[0,1]$; write $F$ for the driver-cost CDF.
Rider and incumbent delay factors are $\beta,\delta\in(0,1]$; $\tau\ge0$ is
the outer pickup-cost slope and $\bar s\ge1$ is the search cap.

\subsection{What is, and is not, nested}

Regimes $\regI$ and $\regA$ are two supply benchmarks: one has no new cohort;
the other has time-homogeneous replenishment.  They should not be called
nested mechanisms unless the platform can suppress the core broadcast.
Their policy domains coincide, while expanded search reproduces the
fixed-arrival technology exactly at $s=1$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P_{\regI}=\mathcal P_{\regA}
&=\{(p_1,p_2):0\le p_1\le p_2\le1\},\\
\mathcal P_{\regE}
&=\{(p_1,p_2,s):0\le p_1\le p_2\le1,\ 1\le s\le\bar s\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Within every regime, $p_2=p_1$ remains the flat-price restriction.  All
comparisons below re-optimize the available policy; no common fixed $p_1$ is imposed.
On an active continuation branch the computation restricts $p_2\le\beta$
without loss, since no rider can obtain positive delayed surplus above $\beta$;
the inactive branch $p_1\ge\beta$ is checked separately.

\section{A complete cutoff-WPBE sits inside every design problem}

\takeaway{The platform commits first.  Every candidate policy $\mu_r$ induces
driver strategies, rider continuation, and Bayesian beliefs; the platform
compares policies only after solving that full cutoff-WPBE correspondence.}

\subsection*{Terminal supply objects}
Let $a\in[0,p_1]$ be the first-window cutoff (accept iff $c\le a$), write
$[x]_+=\max\{x,0\}$, and let $j=1$ denote repeat, $j=2$ rescue.
Conditional on universal rejection, retained incumbent
volunteer intensity is
\[
I_j^\omega(a)=\omega m[F(p_j)-F(a)]_+.
\]
Fresh volunteer intensities in regime $r$ are
\[
e_j^{\regI}=0,\qquad e_j^{\regA}=mF(p_j),
\]
\[
e_1^{\regE}=mF(p_1),\qquad
e_2^{\regE}=mF(p_2)+m\!\int_1^s F(p_2-d(u))\,du.
\]
Here $d(u)\ge0$ is the post-assignment outer pickup cost defined on page 6.
For every regime,
\[
\Lambda_j^r(a)=I_j^\omega(a)+e_j^r,\qquad
C_j^r(a)=1-e^{-\Lambda_j^r(a)}.
\tag{7}
\]
$\Lambda_j^r$ is the expected total willing-driver count, not a per-$m$ rate.
The rider compares $0$, $C_1^r(\beta v-p_1)$, and
$C_2^r(\beta v-p_2)$.  Let $\eta_j^r(a)$ be the resulting action probability
conditional on posting and first-window failure.
\end{minipage}

\subsection{Pure cutoff-WPBE test}

Let $h(z)=(1-e^{-z})/z$ for $z>0$ and $h(0)=1$.  A type-$c$ incumbent's
accept-minus-wait payoff under a proposed aggregate cutoff is
\[
D_r(c;a)=h(mF(a))(p_1-c)
-\delta\omega e^{-mF(a)}
\sum_{j=1}^2\eta_j^r(a)h(\Lambda_j^r(a))[p_j-c]_+.
\tag{8}
\]
Together with the rider and fresh-driver strategies above, $a$ is a cutoff-WPBE
iff on-path beliefs obey Bayes' rule and
\[
D_r(c;a)\ge0\quad\forall c<a,
\qquad
D_r(c;a)\le0\quad\forall c>a,
\tag{9}
\]
with the marginal type indifferent at an interior cutoff.  Because $D_r$ is
piecewise affine in $c$, the solver checks the entire type domain at its affine
interval endpoints, not on a sampled type grid.

\subsection{Outer mechanism design}

Ex-ante completion and expected outer contacts are
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_r(\mu_r,a)&=(1-p_1)\!\left[1-e^{-mF(a)}
+e^{-mF(a)}\{\eta_1^r(a)C_1^r(a)+\eta_2^r(a)C_2^r(a)\}\right],\\
Q^O_{\regE}(\mu_{\regE},a)&=(1-p_1)e^{-mF(a)}\eta_2^{\regE}(a)m(s-1),
\qquad Q^O_{\regI}=Q^O_{\regA}=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Let $B>0$ be value per completion, $\kappa\ge0$ the incremental outer-contact
cost, $\theta=(m,\beta,\delta;\tau,\omega,\bar s,B,\kappa)$, and
$\mathcal E_r^{\WPBE}(\mu_r;\theta)$ the set of cutoffs satisfying (9).
For $J_r=BM_r-\kappa Q_r^O$, the target design problem is
\[
V_r(\theta)=
\max_{\mu_r\in\mathcal P_r}
\min_{a\in\mathcal E_r^{\WPBE}(\mu_r;\theta)}J_r(\mu_r,a),
\qquad
\text{fix }p_1\ \longrightarrow\ \max_{p_2,s}\ \longrightarrow\ \max_{p_1}.
\tag{11}
\]
Conservative equilibrium selection minimizes the same $J_r$ used outside.
Reported candidates combine this nested profile with an independent
global-basin search and dense WPBE recertification.
\endgroup

\section{Fixed footprint: what do fresh arrivals change?}

\takeaway{Regimes $\regI$ and $\regA$ give the platform the same price menu
$(p_1,p_2)$.  Their only difference is whether a new, independent core cohort
can first see the order after first-window failure.}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=.99\textwidth]{../figures/figure_arrival_value.pdf}
\caption{Incumbents only versus fixed footprint with time-homogeneous fresh
arrivals.  Every point is the best cross-checked candidate from the nested
profile and an independent global-basin search, with its complete cutoff-WPBE
re-solved.  Main slice:
$(\beta,\delta,\omega)=(.8,.8,.8)$.}
\label{fig:arrival-value}
\end{figure}

The supply decomposition is deliberately minimal:
\[
\Lambda_j^{\regI}(a)=\omega m[F(p_j)-F(a)]_+,
\qquad
\Lambda_j^{\regA}(a)=\Lambda_j^{\regI}(a)+mF(p_j).
\tag{12}
\]
The first term is the cost-screened set of rejectors who remain in the market;
the second is the new core cohort after price-based thinning.  Setting
$\omega=.8$ means the plotted benchmark has genuine incumbent exit: twenty
percent of rejectors become unavailable before the terminal action.

Fresh arrivals have three equilibrium effects.  They directly increase
terminal coverage; their lottery competition lowers an incumbent's chance of
winning after waiting; and the resulting coverage changes which riders choose
repeat or rescue.  Therefore the green curve is not obtained by adding
$mF(p_j)$ to an old fixed-price outcome.  Both prices and the first-window
cutoff are re-solved at every $m$.

\noindent\colorbox{softgreen}{\parbox{.965\textwidth}{\small
\textbf{Identification.}  $V_{\regA}-V_{\regI}$ is the completion value of
time-homogeneous replenishment at a fixed geographic footprint.  It is not yet
the value of searching farther away.}}

\section{Expanded search adds only an outer annulus}

\takeaway{The core cohort remains exactly the one in regime $\regA$.
Expanded rescue changes only who outside that core can first learn about the
failed order; $s$ is an area multiplier, not an arrival probability.}

\begin{minipage}[t]{.425\textwidth}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=.98\textwidth]{../figures/figure_search_geometry.pdf}
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{.535\textwidth}
Normalize the core radius to $R_0$.  A committed multiplier $s\ge1$ means
\[
R(s)=R_0\sqrt{s},\qquad
N_2^O(s)\sim\operatorname{Pois}((s-1)m),
\tag{13}
\]
where $N_2^O(s)$ is independent of the core cohort $N_2^C$.  Equivalently, the
combined fresh pool has mean $sm$, and its core component is obtained by
ordinary Poisson thinning with share $1/s$.

Repeat uses core reach $s_1=1$.  Only the announced rescue branch uses
$s_2=s$.
\end{minipage}

\subsection{Driver-paid pickup friction occurs after assignment}

Let $\ell$ be driver--order pickup distance and
$u=(\ell/R_0)^2\in[1,s]$ the outer driver's area rank.  Core pickup is
absorbed into persistent cost $c$; an outer winner additionally pays
\[
d(u)=\tau(\sqrt u-1)_+.
\tag{14}
\]
The fresh driver knows $u,p_2,c$ before volunteering but pays $d(u)$ only when
selected.  Her rescue response payoff is
\[
h(\Lambda_2^{\regE}(a))[p_2-c-d(u)].
\]
Because the same positive assignment probability multiplies revenue and cost,
\[
\text{volunteer}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c+d(u)\le p_2.
\tag{15}
\]
Spatial independent increments plus mark thinning therefore give rescue
outer-volunteer intensity
\[
e_O(p_2,s)=m\int_1^sF(p_2-d(u))\,du,
\qquad
\frac{\partial e_O}{\partial s}=mF(p_2-d(s)).
\tag{16}
\]
For $F=U[0,1]$, increasingly distant rings contribute fewer volunteers and
eventually none.  There is no notification-time relocation cost and hence no
fresh-entry fixed point in this maintained benchmark.

\noindent\colorbox{softblue}{\parbox{.965\textwidth}{\small
\textbf{Separation of roles.}  $p_2$ changes willingness among all reached
drivers.  $s$ changes who can see rescue.  Driver pickup cost $d(u)$ thins
willing supply, but it is not a platform cost of sending notifications.}}

\clearpage
\section{A platform search cost makes $s=1$ an economic choice}

\takeaway{A completion-only objective with free contacts asks for a maximal
coverage frontier and often pushes $s$ to its cap.  To ask whether expansion is
worth doing, the objective must price the incremental outer contacts.}

\begin{center}
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabularx}{.92\textwidth}{@{}L{2cm}L{2.65cm}Y@{}}
\toprule
Object & Who bears it? & Economic role\\
\midrule
$d(u)$ & Assigned outer driver & Enters the response threshold and thins willing supply.\\
$\kappa$ per outer contact & Platform/system & Prices notification attention and incremental dispatch capacity.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The expanded-search rescue branch is executed ex ante with probability
\[
q_R^{\regE}=(1-p_1)e^{-mF(a)}\eta_2^{\regE}(a).
\]
Thus expected extra contacts and the maintained design objective are
\[
Q_{\regE}^O=q_R^{\regE}m(s-1),
\qquad
J_{\regE}^{\kappa}=BM_{\regE}-\kappa Q_{\regE}^O.
\tag{17}
\]
This is completion net of notification opportunity cost, not platform profit;
calling it profit would require separate rider fares and driver wages.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=.98\textwidth]{../figures/figure_search_decision.pdf}
\caption{Free-search frontier versus the costly-search design.  Both lines
cross-search $(p_1,p_2,s)$ and re-solve the inner cutoff-WPBE at each $m$.
The main slice uses $(\beta,\delta)=(.8,.8)$, $B=1$, $\kappa=.0125$,
$\tau=.25$, $\omega=.8$, and $\bar s=4$; $s=1$ exactly reproduces
fixed-footprint arrivals.}
\label{fig:search-decision}
\end{figure}

The decline in $s^*(m)$ is not imposed.  As the optimized core becomes more
reliable, the marginal completion value of another ring falls while its
expected contact cost remains.  The outer design can therefore select a cap,
an interior multiplier, or no expansion $s^*=1$.

\section{Market conditions and the research boundary}

\takeaway{Fresh arrivals matter most in an intermediate core market.  Costly
geographic expansion is most valuable when the core is thin and can optimally
switch off once fixed-footprint rescue is reliable.}

Layer & What it identifies & Maintained cost normalization\\

If activation of the core cohort is itself a platform choice, let
$\kappa_C,\kappa_O\ge0$ be the core- and outer-contact costs.  Suppressing
regime superscripts, the symmetric extension is
\[
J_{\kappa_C,\kappa_O}=BM-\kappa_CQ^C-\kappa_OQ^O,
\qquad
Q^C=(1-p_1)e^{-mF(a)}\{\eta_1(a)+\eta_2(a)\}m.
\tag{18}
\]
That version can ask whether to notify fresh core drivers at all.  The current
group-meeting comparison instead isolates two questions: first the supply
value of replenishment, then the economic scope of additional search.

\begin{itemize}
\item Incumbent exit is active through $\omega=.8$ in the main numerics and
enters both terminal supply and the focal rejector's waiting value.
\item Only the $s=1$ slice of the expanded-search technology reproduces
$\regA$; $\regI$ versus $\regA$ is explicitly a supply benchmark comparison.
\item The peak theorem is deliberately moved out of the main presentation.
The next theory step is to characterize the arrival-value peak and the
cost-dependent no-search boundary under stable WPBE branches.
\end{itemize}
