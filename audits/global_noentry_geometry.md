# Global no-entry menu geometry

This note solves the global menu problem in the uniform no-entry baseline.  It
is written independently of the manuscript and does not edit it.

Throughout the main analysis,

\[
 m>0,\qquad 0<\alpha\leq 1,\qquad \gamma=0,\qquad
 0<\beta<1.
\]

The policy is denoted by \((p,q)=(p_1,p_2)\), with
\(0\leq p\leq q\leq1\).  The main comparison theorem below assumes
\(\beta\geq 1/2\), which includes the equality case omitted by the current
draft.  The degenerate boundary \(\alpha=0\) is recorded separately.

## 1. Reduced primitives

Put

\[
 k:=\alpha m,\qquad
 C(x):=1-e^{-kx},\qquad
 h(a):=
 \begin{cases}
 (e^{ma}-1)/a,&a>0,\\
 m,&a=0.
 \end{cases}
\]

Two elementary facts used repeatedly are

\[
 h(a)=\int_0^m e^{ta}\,dt\geq m,
 \qquad
 0<\frac{h'(a)}{h(a)}<m                         \tag{1}
\]

for \(a\geq0\), where the derivative at zero is the right derivative and
\(h'(0)/h(0)=m/2\).  The logarithmic derivative in (1) is the mean of
\(t\in[0,m]\) under weights proportional to \(e^{ta}\).

Fix a candidate first-period cutoff \(a\in[0,p]\).  When \(p<q<\beta\),
define

\[
 A_z(a):=(\beta-z)C(z-a),\qquad z\in\{p,q\},
 \qquad B_p:=\beta(1-p).                         \tag{2}
\]

The rider's repeat-versus-rescue threshold is below one precisely when

\[
 A_q(a)>A_p(a).                                  \tag{3}
\]

Indeed, if \(C_j=C(p_j-a)\), the threshold

\[
 v^M(a)=\frac{qC_q-pC_p}{\beta(C_q-C_p)}
\]

satisfies \(v^M(a)<1\) if and only if
\((\beta-q)C_q>(\beta-p)C_p\).  At equality in (3), the marginal rider is
\(v=1\), so rescue has zero mass.  Thus the conditional continuation coverage
chosen by posting riders is exactly

\[
 K_{p,q}(a)
 =\frac{\max\{A_p(a),A_q(a)\}}{B_p}.             \tag{4}
\]

To verify (4), in the active-rescue region substitute \(v^M\) into

\[
 \frac{C_p(v^M-p/\beta)+C_q(1-v^M)}{1-p}.
\]

All terms involving \(C_p\) cancel, leaving

\[
 K_{p,q}(a)=
 \frac{1-q/\beta}{1-p}C(q-a)
 =\frac{A_q(a)}{B_p}.                             \tag{5}
\]

In the repeat-only region the same object is \(A_p(a)/B_p\).  Formula (4)
also shows explicitly why the repeat-versus-abandon tie is substantive but
does not create an omitted mass: at the kink the two expressions coincide.

Multiplying the cutoff driver's accept-minus-wait payoff by the positive
factor \(me^{ma}\) gives the sign-equivalent scalar

\[
 D_{p,q}(a):=(p-a)h(a)-K_{p,q}(a).                \tag{6}
\]

Thus the cutoff conditions are

\[
 \begin{array}{lll}
 a=0&\Longleftrightarrow&D_{p,q}(0)\leq0,\\
 0<a<p&\Longleftrightarrow&D_{p,q}(a)=0,\\
 a=p&\Longleftrightarrow&D_{p,q}(p)\geq0.
 \end{array}                                      \tag{7}
\]

The last line is important: at the upper boundary, the cutoff type must not
prefer waiting.  Since her accept-now payoff is zero and her wait payoff is
nonnegative, an upper-boundary equilibrium necessarily has equality.

### The repeat branch is never an interior equilibrium

For every \(0<p<\beta\) and \(0\leq a<p\),

\[
 (p-a)h(a)-\frac{A_p(a)}{B_p}>0.                 \tag{8}
\]

This is the usual flat-policy strict inequality.  In the original notation it
is

\[
 (p-a)m e^{ma}
 \left[\phi(ma)-e^{-ma}\alpha\rho(p)
 \phi(k(p-a))\right]>0,
\quad
 \rho(p)=\frac{\beta-p}{\beta(1-p)}<1.
\]

For \(a>0\), use \(\phi(ma)>e^{-ma}\),
\(\alpha\rho(p)\phi(k(p-a))\leq1\).  At \(a=0\), strictness follows from
\(p>0\), \(\rho(p)<1\).  Consequently, every root of (6) under a strict
menu \(p<q<\beta\) lies strictly in the active-rescue region (3).  The rider
kink itself cannot be an equilibrium cutoff.

## 2. Audit of the completion identity

Let

\[
 M(p,q;a)
 =(1-p)\left[1-e^{-ma}+e^{-ma}K_{p,q}(a)\right]. \tag{9}
\]

### Proposition 1 (identity, qualifications, and ordering)

If \(a\in(0,p)\) is a cutoff equilibrium, then

\[
 \boxed{M(p,q;a)=(1-p)mp\phi(ma).}               \tag{10}
\]

The same identity holds at an equilibrium \(a=p>0\).  At a reject-all
equilibrium \(a=0\), instead,

\[
 M(p,q;0)=(1-p)K_{p,q}(0)\geq(1-p)mp,            \tag{11}
\]

with equality if and only if the cost-zero driver is indifferent.

**Proof.**  Interior indifference is

\[
 e^{-ma}K_{p,q}(a)=m\phi(ma)(p-a).
\]

Since \(1-e^{-ma}=ma\phi(ma)\), substituting into (9) gives (10).  At
\(a=p\), equilibrium requires the nonnegative waiting payoff of the cutoff
type to be zero, so \(K_{p,q}(p)=0\), and (10) follows again.  At \(a=0\),
condition (7) is \(K_{p,q}(0)\geq mp\), which gives (11).  \(\square\)

Because \(\phi(ma)\) is strictly decreasing in \(a>0\), (10) is strictly
decreasing in the positive equilibrium cutoff.  Moreover every positive
equilibrium gives less than \((1-p)mp\), whereas a zero-cutoff equilibrium
gives at least that much.  Therefore, without using any uniqueness claim:

* if a policy has positive equilibrium cutoffs, its pessimistic outcome is
  generated by the largest positive cutoff;
* a zero-cutoff branch can never be the pessimistic branch when a positive
  branch coexists;
* if zero is the only cutoff, its actual value (11), not the right side of
  (10), must be used.

Thus the proposed identity is valid, but only with these boundary
qualifications.

## 3. A global one-crossing theorem: strict menus have one cutoff

The following result removes the possible multiplicity left open by the
identity audit.

### Lemma 2 (all active-rescue zeros cross downward)

Fix \(0\leq p<q<\beta\).  Every zero
\(a\in[0,p)\) of the active-rescue expression in (6) has a strictly negative
right/ordinary derivative.

**Proof.**  Write

\[
 u=p-a,\qquad z=q-a,\qquad b=\beta-q,
 \qquad B=B_p=\beta(1-p).
\]

At an active-rescue zero,

\[
 uh(a)=\frac bB C(z).                             \tag{12}
\]

Since

\[
 B-b=q-\beta p>0,
\]

(12) and \(h(a)\geq m\) imply

\[
 u<\frac{C(z)}m.                                  \tag{13}
\]

Consider the ratio \(R(a)=u h(a)/C(z)\).  Its logarithmic derivative is

\[
 \frac{R'(a)}{R(a)}
 =-\frac1u+\frac{h'(a)}{h(a)}
   +\frac{k}{e^{kz}-1}.                           \tag{14}
\]

By (13), \(k=\alpha m\), and \(0<\alpha\leq1\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac1u-\frac{k}{e^{kz}-1}
 &>\frac{m}{1-e^{-kz}}-
      \frac{k e^{-kz}}{1-e^{-kz}}\\
 &=m\frac{1-\alpha e^{-kz}}{1-e^{-kz}}
 \geq m
 >\frac{h'(a)}{h(a)}.
\end{aligned}                                     \tag{15}
\]

Hence (14) is strictly negative.  Since at a zero \(R=b/B\), this is exactly
the sign of the derivative of (6).  When \(p>0\), the argument remains valid
at \(a=0\) using the right derivatives \(h(0)=m\),
\(h'(0)/h(0)=m/2\).  When \(p=0\), the interval \([0,p)\) is empty and the
zero-crossing claim is vacuous.  \(\square\)

### Theorem 3 (unique cutoff for every no-entry menu)

For \(0\leq p<q<\beta\):

1. if \(D_{p,q}(0)\leq0\), the unique cutoff is \(a=0\);
2. if \(D_{p,q}(0)>0\), there is a unique cutoff
   \(a\in(0,p)\).

In particular, the equality case \(D_{p,q}(0)=0\) does **not** coexist with a
positive root.

**Proof.**  By (8), any zero is strictly in the active-rescue region, so Lemma
2 applies.  Also

\[
 D_{p,q}(p)=-\frac{(\beta-q)C(q-p)}{B_p}<0.       \tag{16}
\]

A continuous scalar function for which every zero crosses strictly downward
can have at most one zero: two downward crossings would require an intervening
upward crossing.  If \(D(0)>0\), (16) and the intermediate value theorem give
one interior zero.  If \(D(0)<0\), an interior zero would require a prior
upward crossing.  If \(D(0)=0\), Lemma 2 makes \(D<0\) immediately to the
right, and the same argument excludes a later zero.  The boundary condition
(7) completes the proof.  \(\square\)

This theorem is stronger than what pessimistic selection requires.  In the
uniform no-entry baseline every on-path menu has a unique anonymous symmetric
pure cutoff, so conservative selection collapses to that cutoff.

### Other menu regimes and boundary cases

* **Flat menu \(q=p>0\).**  Inequality (8) gives the unique cutoff \(a=p\).
* **Repeat-only menu \(0<p<\beta\leq q\).**  Rescue has zero mass for every
  candidate cutoff.  Inequality (8) again gives the unique cutoff \(a=p\).
* **No-continuation first payment \(p\geq\beta\).**  No positive mass of
  riders has nonnegative delayed surplus.  The unique cutoff is \(a=p\), and
  every \(q\geq p\) has value \((1-p)(1-e^{-mp})\).
* **Zero first payment \(p=0\).**  The cutoff set is the singleton \(\{0\}\)
  by feasibility.  If \(0<q<\beta\), value is
  \((1-q/\beta)(1-e^{-kq})\); it is zero at \(q=0\) and for \(q\geq\beta\).
* **No survival \(\alpha=0\).**  There is no period-2 accepting supply.  For
  \(p>0\), the unique cutoff is \(p\) under every menu and the dynamic value
  equals the flat value.  At \(p=0\), the only feasible cutoff is zero and
  completion is zero.  All formulas involving division by \(k\) below are
  therefore stated only for \(\alpha>0\).
* **\(p=1\).**  Ex ante completion is zero.  This policy cannot be optimal
  when \(m>0\).

## 4. Rider-kink and implementability geometry at fixed \(p\)

Fix \(0<p<\beta\) for this section.

### 4.1 The rider-threshold kink

For a strict menu \(p<q<\beta\), the difference

\[
 \Delta_{p,q}(a):=A_q(a)-A_p(a)
\]

is strictly increasing in \(a\), because

\[
 \Delta'_{p,q}(a)
 =k e^{ka}\left[(\beta-p)e^{-kp}-(\beta-q)e^{-kq}\right]>0. \tag{17}
\]

Also \(\Delta_{p,q}(p)>0\).  If its zero lies in \([0,p]\), it is

\[
 r(p,q)=\frac1k\log\left(
 \frac{q-p}{(\beta-p)e^{-kp}-(\beta-q)e^{-kq}}
 \right).                                        \tag{18}
\]

Below \(r\), riders repeat only; at \(r\), the rider threshold is exactly
one; above \(r\), a positive mass rescues.  If the number in (18) is negative,
rescue is active for all \(a\in[0,p]\).  By (8), neither the repeat-only
region nor the kink can contain an equilibrium.  Every strict-menu equilibrium
is strictly above the kink.

### 4.2 Rescue envelope

For a target cutoff \(a<\beta\), maximize

\[
 A(a,q):=(\beta-q)C(q-a),\qquad q\in[a,\beta].   \tag{19}
\]

The function is strictly concave in \(q\), since

\[
 A_{qq}(a,q)
 =-e^{-k(q-a)}\bigl[2k+k^2(\beta-q)\bigr]<0.
\]

Its unique maximizer \(Q(a)\in(a,\beta)\) is characterized by

\[
 \boxed{e^{k(Q(a)-a)}-1=k(\beta-Q(a)).}          \tag{20}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 Q(a)=\beta-
 \frac{W_0(e^{1+k(\beta-a)})-1}{k}.              \tag{21}
\]

The strict inequality \(e^x-1>x\) in (20) gives

\[
 a<Q(a)<\frac{a+\beta}{2}.                       \tag{22}
\]

Let

\[
 S(a):=A(a,Q(a)).                                 \tag{23}
\]

Writing \(y(a)=\beta-Q(a)\), (20) yields the useful closed forms

\[
 S(a)=\frac{k y(a)^2}{1+k y(a)},
 \qquad
 S'(a)=-C(Q(a)-a),
 \qquad
 \frac{S'(a)}{S(a)}=-\frac1{\beta-Q(a)}.         \tag{24}
\]

For fixed \(p\), the constrained rescue envelope is

\[
 \overline A_p(a)=
 \max_{q\in[p,\beta]}A(a,q)
 =A\bigl(a,\max\{p,Q(a)\}\bigr).                \tag{25}
\]

Define

\[
 \overline D_p(a)
 =(p-a)h(a)-\frac{\overline A_p(a)}{B_p}.         \tag{26}
\]

Every zero of (26) has \(Q(a)>p\); otherwise (26) is the strictly positive
flat expression (8).  At such a zero, the proof of Lemma 2 applies.  A slightly
sharper envelope version is also available: the envelope first-order condition
and the root equality imply

\[
 p-a\leq \frac{\alpha(\beta-Q(a))}
 {1+k(\beta-Q(a))},
\]

which makes the logarithmic derivative strictly negative directly.  Hence
\(\overline D_p\) has at most one zero, and every zero crosses downward.

### 4.3 Exact implementable-cutoff correspondence

For a target \(a\in(0,p)\), strict rescue prices implementing it solve

\[
 \boxed{A(a,q)=B_p(p-a)h(a).}                    \tag{27}
\]

This equation cannot create a repeat-only or rider-kink pseudo-implementer.
By the strict flat inequality (8), its right side is strictly larger than
\(A(a,p)\).  Every solution therefore has \(A(a,q)>A(a,p)\), which is
exactly the strictly active-rescue condition.

At \(q=p\), the left side is strictly below the right side by (8), and at
\(q=\beta\) it is zero.  Therefore:

* if \(\overline A_p(a)<B_p(p-a)h(a)\), cutoff \(a\) is not implementable;
* if equality holds, the unique implementing rescue is \(q=Q(a)>p\);
* if the envelope is strictly larger, exactly two prices
  \(p<q_-(a)<Q(a)<q_+(a)<\beta\) implement \(a\).

Both prices in the last item induce \(a\) as the **unique** equilibrium cutoff
by Theorem 3.  This is not merely a list of roots of a necessary equation.

At \(a=0\) and \(p>0\), reject-all is implemented exactly by

\[
 A(0,q)\geq B_pmp.                                \tag{28}
\]

At \(a=p\), the implementing menus are the flat price \(q=p\) and all
repeat-only prices \(q\geq\beta\).  A strict active-rescue menu never
implements \(a=p\).

## 5. Complete solution of the fixed-\(p\) problem

Let

\[
 Q_0:=Q(0),\qquad S_0:=S(0).
\]

By (22), \(Q_0<\beta/2<1/2\).  Define

\[
 \boxed{
 p_z:=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4S_0/(\beta m)}}2.}        \tag{29}
\]

This number is real and satisfies

\[
 0<p_z<Q_0<\frac12.                               \tag{30}
\]

To prove this, apply the flat strict inequality (8) at \(p=Q_0,a=0\):

\[
 S_0<\beta mQ_0(1-Q_0)<\frac{\beta m}{4}.       \tag{31}
\]

The smaller root in (29) is therefore well defined, and monotonicity of
\(p(1-p)\) on \([0,1/2]\) gives \(p_z<Q_0\).

The threshold \(p_z\) separates the reject-all and positive-cutoff portions of
the fixed-price optimum.

### Theorem 4 (fixed-first-payment optimizer)

For \(H_m(p)=\max_{q\in[p,1]}\underline M_m^{\alpha,0}(p,q)\):

#### (i) Reject-all optimum: \(0\leq p\leq p_z\)

The unique optimal rescue payment is

\[
 q^*(p)=Q_0,
\]

the unique cutoff is \(a^*(p)=0\), and

\[
 \boxed{H_m(p)=\frac{S_0}{\beta}.}               \tag{32}
\]

For \(0<p<p_z\), condition (28) is strict at \(Q_0\); at \(p=p_z\) it holds
with equality.  Thus the boundary case \(D(0)=0\) belongs to the reject-all
region and, by Theorem 3, has no hidden positive equilibrium.

#### (ii) Positive-cutoff optimum: \(p_z<p<\beta\)

There is a unique \(a^*(p)\in(0,p)\) solving

\[
 \boxed{
 (p-a)h(a)=\frac{S(a)}{\beta(1-p)}.}             \tag{33}
\]

The unique optimal rescue is

\[
 \boxed{q^*(p)=Q(a^*(p))\in(p,\beta),}           \tag{34}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 H_m(p)=(1-p)mp\phi(ma^*(p)).}                   \tag{35}
\]

#### (iii) Repeat-only optimum: \(\beta\leq p\leq1\)

Every \(q\in[p,1]\) gives the same value,

\[
 \boxed{H_m(p)=(1-p)(1-e^{-mp}).}                \tag{36}
\]

In particular, the fixed-\(p\) supremum is a maximum for every
\(p\in[0,1]\).

**Proof.**  First consider \(0<p<\beta\).  If \(p\leq Q_0\), the largest
possible left side of (28) is \(S_0\).  Equations (29)--(31) show that (28)
can hold for some \(q\) exactly when \(p\leq p_z\).  If \(p>Q_0\), the
constrained maximum of \(A(0,q)\) occurs at \(q=p\), and the flat strict
inequality rules out (28).  Hence the fixed-price optimum can have cutoff zero
exactly in the first regime.

For \(p<p_z\), any positive-cutoff menu has, by Proposition 1,

\[
 M<(1-p)mp<S_0/\beta,
\]

and any zero-cutoff menu has \(M=A(0,q)/\beta\leq S_0/\beta\), with equality
only at \(Q_0\).  The same proof works at \(p=p_z\), with the first inequality
weak at zero but strict for every positive cutoff.  This proves (i), including
uniqueness.

Now take \(p_z<p<\beta\).  Then \(\overline D_p(0)>0\), while
\(\overline D_p(p)<0\).  The envelope one-crossing result gives a unique root
\(a^*(p)\), which is (33).  For an arbitrary strict rescue \(q\), let \(a_q\)
be its unique equilibrium cutoff.  At a positive root,

\[
 (p-a_q)h(a_q)=\frac{A(a_q,q)}{B_p}
 \leq\frac{\overline A_p(a_q)}{B_p},
\]

so \(\overline D_p(a_q)\leq0\).  Because the envelope is positive below its
unique downward crossing, \(a_q\geq a^*(p)\).  Flat and repeat-only menus have
cutoff \(p\), so the same inequality holds.  Proposition 1 then implies that
completion is maximized by the smallest cutoff.  The unique price attaining
the envelope at \(a^*(p)\) is \(Q(a^*(p))\), and Theorem 3 makes
\(a^*(p)\) its unique equilibrium.  This proves (ii).  Part (iii) follows from
the boundary-regime analysis after Theorem 3.  \(\square\)

### Implementable cutoff sets, summarized

The proof gives the entire correspondence, not just the optimizer:

* if \(0<p\leq p_z\), every \(a\in[0,p]\) is implementable; zero is
  implemented by the superlevel interval in (28), positive \(a<p\) by the two
  prices solving (27), and \(p\) by \(q=p\) or \(q\geq\beta\);
* if \(p_z<p<\beta\), the implementable set is
  \([a^*(p),p]\); the lower endpoint has the single tangent implementer
  \(Q(a^*(p))\), every \(a\in(a^*(p),p)\) has two strict-rescue implementers,
  and \(p\) has the flat/repeat-only implementers;
* for \(p=0\), only cutoff zero is feasible;
* for \(p\geq\beta\), only cutoff \(p\) is relevant.

## 6. The lower quadratic branch is a theorem

The fixed-\(p\) solution can be inverted exactly.  This both settles the
quadratic-branch ambiguity and reduces the outer problem to one scalar.

For \(a\in[0,\beta)\), define

\[
 R(a):=\frac{S(a)}{\beta h(a)}.                   \tag{37}
\]

Equation (33) is

\[
 (1-p)(p-a)=R(a).                                 \tag{38}
\]

### Lemma 5 (only the minus branch is feasible)

For every \(a\in[0,\beta)\), equation (38) has a unique solution in
\((a,Q(a))\).  It is

\[
 \boxed{
 P(a)=\frac{1+a-\sqrt{(1-a)^2-4R(a)}}2.}         \tag{39}
\]

In particular, the plus quadratic branch can never be a fixed-\(p\) rescue
optimum.

**Proof.**  Let \(g_a(p)=(1-p)(p-a)\).  By (22),
\(Q(a)<(1+a)/2\), and \(g_a\) is strictly increasing on
\((a,(1+a)/2)\).  At \(p=Q(a)\), apply the strict flat inequality (8) with
candidate cutoff \(a<Q(a)\):

\[
 h(a)(Q(a)-a)>\frac{S(a)}{\beta(1-Q(a))}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 g_a(Q(a))>R(a),
\]

whereas \(g_a(a)=0<R(a)\).  There is therefore exactly one solution between
\(a\) and \(Q(a)\), and because that interval lies below the vertex of the
quadratic, it is its minus root (39).  This also proves that the discriminant
is positive.  \(\square\)

The function \(P\) is continuous, satisfies

\[
 P(0)=p_z,\qquad a<P(a)<Q(a)<\beta,
 \qquad \lim_{a\uparrow\beta}P(a)=\beta,         \tag{40}
\]

and is strictly increasing.  One clean proof of monotonicity is structural.
For every \(a>0\), policy \((P(a),Q(a))\) has \(a\) as the unique envelope
root.  If \(P(a_1)=P(a_2)\) for distinct positive arguments, the same
fixed-\(p\) envelope would have two zeros, contradicting the
downward-crossing theorem.  The endpoint obeys the same logic: if
\(P(a)=P(0)=p_z\) for some \(a>0\), the fixed-\(p_z\) envelope would have
zeros at both zero and \(a\).  Thus continuous \(P\) is injective.  The
endpoint limits in (40) force it to be increasing.  Consequently,

\[
 P:[0,\beta)\longrightarrow[p_z,\beta)
\]

is a bijection, and its inverse is precisely \(p\mapsto a^*(p)\).

## 7. Outer optimization and policy-value attainment

Extend \(P(\beta)=\beta\), and define the continuous function

\[
 J(a):=
 \begin{cases}
 (1-P(a))P(a)\dfrac{1-e^{-ma}}a,&a>0,\\[6pt]
 m p_z(1-p_z)=S_0/\beta,&a=0.
 \end{cases}                                      \tag{41}
\]

At the other endpoint,

\[
 J(\beta)=(1-\beta)(1-e^{-m\beta}).              \tag{42}
\]

Equations (35), (39), and the bijection above show that \(J\) is exactly the
fixed-price value on \([p_z,\beta]\), expressed in the equilibrium-cutoff
coordinate.  The plateau \(p\in[0,p_z]\) has the common value \(J(0)\), and
the region \(p\geq\beta\) has the flat value (36).  Thus, without any
unproved uniqueness or single-peakedness claim,

\[
 D_{\alpha,0}^*(m)
 =\max\left\{
 J(0),\ \max_{a\in[0,\beta]}J(a),\
 \max_{p\in[\beta,1]}(1-p)(1-e^{-mp})
 \right\}.                                       \tag{43}
\]

The repeated \(J(0)\) in (43) only emphasizes the plateau; it can of course
be omitted.  All sets are compact and all displayed functions are continuous.
Therefore the original policy supremum is attained.

### Strict escalation when \(\beta\geq1/2\)

Let

\[
 F_m(p)=(1-p)(1-e^{-mp}).
\]

Its unique maximizer \(p_F(m)\) solves

\[
 e^{mp_F}-1=m(1-p_F),                             \tag{44}
\]

and satisfies \(0<p_F(m)<1/2\): at \(p=1/2\),
\(e^{m/2}-1>m/2\), so the unique root of (44) is smaller.

For every \(0<p<\beta\), every strict menu \(p<q<\beta\) has a cutoff
strictly below \(p\).  If it is positive, Proposition 1 and strict decrease of
\(\phi\) give

\[
 M(p,q;a)>(1-p)mp\phi(mp)=F_m(p).
\]

If it is zero, (11) gives the still stronger
\(M\geq(1-p)mp>F_m(p)\).  Hence

\[
 H_m(p)>F_m(p)\qquad(0<p<\beta).                 \tag{45}
\]

When \(\beta\geq1/2\), (44) gives \(p_F<\beta\), including at
\(\beta=1/2\).  Applying (45) at \(p_F\) proves

\[
 \boxed{D_{\alpha,0}^*(m)>F_0^*(m)
 \quad\text{for all }m>0,\ 0<\alpha\leq1,\
 \beta\geq1/2.}                                  \tag{46}
\]

Thus the current sufficient condition extends exactly to \(\beta=1/2\).
More generally, the same proof gives the sufficient condition
\(\beta>p_F(m)\); no necessity claim is made here for smaller \(\beta\).

For \(\beta\geq1/2\), (43) simplifies further.  First,

\[
 J(0)=\left(1-\frac{Q_0}{\beta}\right)
       (1-e^{-\alpha mQ_0})
 <(1-Q_0)(1-e^{-mQ_0})\leq F_0^*(m),             \tag{47}
\]

where strictness uses \(0<Q_0<\beta<1\).  Second, every \(p\geq\beta\)
has flat value at most \(F_0^*(m)\).  By (46), neither boundary region can be
globally optimal.  Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 D_{\alpha,0}^*(m)=\max_{a\in(0,\beta)}J(a),}    \tag{48}
\]

and every global optimizer is a strict menu of the form

\[
 \boxed{(p_1^*,p_2^*)=(P(a^*),Q(a^*))},
 \qquad a^*\in\arg\max_{a\in[0,\beta]}J(a).}     \tag{49}
\]

Conversely every \(a^*\) in that argmax gives a globally optimal menu.  The
argmax is nonempty and compact and, by (47)--(48), lies strictly inside
\((0,\beta)\).

This is an exact optimizer correspondence.  It deliberately does **not**
assert that the argmax is a singleton or that \(J\) is globally
single-peaked.

### First-order condition for the outer argmax

For use in exact or interval-certified computation, all ingredients of the
remaining scalar maximization have explicit derivatives.  From (24),

\[
 R'(a)=-R(a)\left[
 \frac1{\beta-Q(a)}+\frac{h'(a)}{h(a)}
 \right],                                        \tag{50}
\]

and implicit differentiation of (38) gives

\[
 P'(a)=
 \frac{1-P(a)+R'(a)}{1+a-2P(a)}>0.               \tag{51}
\]

Every global maximizer in (49) therefore satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 P'(a)\frac{1-2P(a)}{P(a)(1-P(a))}
 +\frac{m}{e^{ma}-1}-\frac1a=0.}                 \tag{52}
\]

Since the last two terms in (52) have negative sum and \(P'(a)>0\), every
optimizer satisfies

\[
 p_1^*=P(a^*)<\frac12.                            \tag{53}
\]

Equation (52) is necessary, not declared sufficient.  Global optimality is
the exact argmax condition in (48)--(49); this avoids assuming uniqueness or
single-peakedness.

## 8. Main conclusions and cautions

1. The candidate identity \(M=(1-p)mp\phi(ma)\) is correct at every positive
   equilibrium cutoff and at an upper-boundary equilibrium, but not generally
   at \(a=0\).  Reject-all completion is weakly larger than its continuous
   extension.
2. Larger positive cutoffs give strictly worse completion.  More strongly,
   every strict active no-entry menu has only one cutoff, including when
   \(D(0)=0\); pessimistic symmetric-cutoff selection is therefore innocuous
   in this baseline.
3. Repeat-only candidates and the rider-threshold kink cannot be equilibrium
   cutoffs under a strict active menu.
4. For fixed \(p\), reject-all is optimal exactly on \([0,p_z]\); above
   \(p_z\) and below \(\beta\), the optimum is the unique tangent menu
   \(q=Q(a^*(p))\); at and above \(\beta\), escalation is irrelevant.
5. The tangent menu has no larger hidden root.  This follows from the global
   downward-crossing lemma, not from numerical evidence.
6. The quadratic inversion always uses the lower branch \(P(a)\); the upper
   branch lies beyond the feasible envelope maximizer and cannot solve the
   fixed-price problem.
7. The full policy value is attained.  For \(\beta\geq1/2\), every optimizer
   is a strict menu parameterized by an interior maximizer of the continuous
   scalar \(J\), and dynamic value strictly exceeds optimized flat value.
8. No uniqueness or global single-peakedness of the final scalar \(J\) has
   been assumed.  Those are separate claims and are unnecessary for policy
   attainment, strict improvement, or the exact optimizer correspondence.

## 9. Full patience region

This section lets \(\beta\) vary while holding \((m,\alpha)\), with
\(m>0\) and \(0<\alpha\leq1\), fixed.  Write

\[
 D(\beta):=D_{\alpha,0}^*(m;\beta),
 \qquad F^*:=F_0^*(m),
\]

where the extra semicolon only makes the dependence on patience visible.

### Lemma 6 (a fixed strict menu is strictly patience-improving)

Fix a strict menu \(0\leq p<q<1\), and let \(W_{p,q}(\beta)\) be its unique
symmetric-cutoff completion value.  Then:

1. \(W_{p,q}(\beta)=F_m(p)\) for \(0<\beta\leq q\);
2. \(W_{p,q}(\beta)\) is strictly increasing on \((q,1)\);
3. it is continuous at \(\beta=q\), and hence continuous and nondecreasing on
   \((0,1)\).

**Proof.**  If \(\beta\leq q\), the rescue price is not used by a positive
mass.  Repeat alone cannot sustain a cutoff below \(p\), so the unique cutoff
is \(p\) and value is \(F_m(p)\).

Now take \(q<\beta_1<\beta_2<1\).  In the active-rescue scalar, the
continuation coefficients are

\[
 \frac{\beta-z}{\beta(1-p)}
 =\frac{1-z/\beta}{1-p},\qquad z\in\{p,q\}.       \tag{54}
\]

They are nondecreasing in \(\beta\), and the rescue coefficient \(z=q>0\)
is strictly increasing.  At the \(\beta_1\)-equilibrium cutoff rescue is
strictly active, so

\[
 D_{p,q}^{\beta_2}(a_{\beta_1})
 <D_{p,q}^{\beta_1}(a_{\beta_1}).                \tag{55}
\]

If \(a_{\beta_1}>0\), the right side is zero.  The unique-zero and
downward-crossing theorem therefore implies that the \(\beta_2\) cutoff is
either zero or is strictly smaller than \(a_{\beta_1}\).  If both cutoffs are
positive, Proposition 1 makes completion strictly larger.  If the new cutoff
is zero, its value is at least \((1-p)mp\), strictly above the old
positive-cutoff value.

If \(a_{\beta_1}=0\), (55) preserves reject-all, and its value is

\[
 W_{p,q}(\beta)=
 \left(1-\frac q\beta\right)C(q),                \tag{56}
\]

which has derivative \(qC(q)/\beta^2>0\).  This proves strict increase on
\((q,1)\).

Finally, as \(\beta\downarrow q\), the continuation coefficient of rescue
vanishes.  If the cutoffs did not converge to \(p\), a subsequential limit
below \(p\) would contradict the strict flat inequality (8).  Proposition 1
then gives \(W_{p,q}(\beta)\to F_m(p)\).  \(\square\)

The strict part of this lemma relies on equilibrium uniqueness.  Merely
knowing that every zero crosses downward would not by itself justify comparing
pessimistically selected branches if multiple roots remained.

### Continuity of the optimized value in patience

The exact scalar reduction proves continuity without appealing to an
unverified general equilibrium-correspondence theorem.  To see this, write
\(a=\beta x\), \(x\in[0,1]\).  For
\(\delta=\beta-a=\beta(1-x)\), let \(y\in[0,\delta]\) solve

\[
 e^{ky}-1=k(\delta-y).                            \tag{57}
\]

The left side minus the right side is strictly increasing in \(y\), so its
unique solution is continuous in \(\delta\), including \(y(0)=0\).  Then

\[
 Q_\beta(a)=a+y,\qquad
 \beta-Q_\beta(a)=\delta-y,
\]

and (23)--(24), (37), (39), and (41) show that

\[
 (\beta,x)\longmapsto J_\beta(\beta x)           \tag{58}
\]

is jointly continuous away from its removable cutoff endpoints.  At
\(x=0\), \(h(0)=m\), the solution \(Q_\beta(0)\), its envelope
\(S_\beta(0)\), and \(P_\beta(0)=p_z(\beta)\) are continuous; the
discriminant is strictly positive by (31).  At \(x=1\),
\(Q_\beta(\beta)=P_\beta(\beta)=\beta\), \(S=R=0\), and
\(J_\beta(\beta)=(1-\beta)(1-e^{-m\beta})\).  These formulas give a joint
continuous extension on every compact subset of
\((0,1)\times[0,1]\).  Similarly, after writing
\(p=\beta+(1-\beta)s\),

\[
 (\beta,s)\longmapsto F_m(\beta+(1-\beta)s)
\]

is jointly continuous on \((0,1)\times[0,1]\).  The exact outer reduction is

\[
 D(\beta)=\max\left\{
 \max_{x\in[0,1]}J_\beta(\beta x),
 \max_{s\in[0,1]}F_m(\beta+(1-\beta)s)
 \right\}.                                       \tag{59}
\]

Uniform continuity on local compact rectangles (the elementary maximum
theorem) therefore gives:

\[
 \boxed{D(\beta)\text{ is continuous on }(0,1).} \tag{60}
\]

### Theorem 7 (the patience region is one upper interval)

Define

\[
 \boxed{
 \beta_c(m,\alpha)
 :=\inf\{\beta\in(0,1):D_{\alpha,0}^*(m;\beta)>F_0^*(m)\}.} \tag{61}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal R
 =\{(m,\alpha,\beta):m>0,\ 0<\alpha\leq1,\
 \ \beta>\beta_c(m,\alpha)\}.}                  \tag{62}
\]

For fixed \((m,\alpha)\),

\[
 D(\beta)=F^*\quad(0<\beta\leq\beta_c),
\]

and \(D(\beta)\) is strictly increasing on
\((\beta_c,1)\).

**Proof.**  Flat menus are always feasible, so \(D(\beta)\geq F^*\).  If
\(D(\beta_1)>F^*\), attainment implies that an optimizing policy is a strict
menu with \(q<\beta_1\): flat and repeat-only menus cannot beat \(F^*\).
For \(\beta_2>\beta_1\), reuse that menu and apply Lemma 6:

\[
 D(\beta_2)\geq W_{p,q}(\beta_2)
 >W_{p,q}(\beta_1)=D(\beta_1)>F^*.               \tag{63}
\]

Thus the strict-gain set is an upper interval, and the same argument shows
strict increase of \(D\) within it.  Continuity (60) makes the strict-gain set
open.  It is nonempty by (45) whenever \(\beta>p_F(m)\).  The bounds proved
below show its lower endpoint is positive.  Hence it is exactly the open
interval in (62), and continuity gives equality with \(F^*\) at the endpoint.
\(\square\)

### Explicit nontrivial bounds on the threshold

Let

\[
 \boxed{
 \beta_L(m):=-\frac1m\log(1-F_0^*(m)).}          \tag{64}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 0<\beta_L(m)\leq\beta_c(m,\alpha)\leq p_F(m)<\frac12.} \tag{65}
\]

For the lower bound, under any active strict menu \(p<q<\beta\), completion
requires at least one initial incumbent with cost at most \(q\).  Since that
count is Poisson with mean \(mq\),

\[
 M(p,q;a)\leq1-e^{-mq}<1-e^{-m\beta}.            \tag{66}
\]

When \(\beta\leq\beta_L\), (64)--(66) rule out a strict improvement; flat or
repeat-only menus also give at most \(F^*\).  Hence \(D(\beta)=F^*\) there.
Moreover,

\[
 F^*=(1-p_F)(1-e^{-mp_F})<1-e^{-mp_F},
\]

so \(0<\beta_L<p_F\).  For the upper bound, if \(\beta>p_F\), choose
\(p=p_F\) and any \(q\in(p_F,\beta)\); (45) gives a strict improvement on
\(F^*\).  This proves (65).

No assertion that either bound is always tight is needed for the threshold
theorem.

### Necessary and sufficient scalar test

Let all objects in (20)--(24), (37), (39), and (41) carry a subscript
\(\beta\), and define

\[
 \mathcal J_{m,\alpha}(\beta)
 :=\max_{a\in[0,\beta]}J_\beta(a).               \tag{67}
\]

The other term in (59) is a restricted flat maximum and is never above
\(F^*\).  Therefore the exact necessary and sufficient condition is

\[
 \boxed{
 D_{\alpha,0}^*(m;\beta)>F_0^*(m)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal J_{m,\alpha}(\beta)>F_0^*(m).}         \tag{68}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 \boxed{
 \beta_c(m,\alpha)
 =\inf\{\beta:\mathcal J_{m,\alpha}(\beta)>F^*\}
 =\sup\{\beta:\mathcal J_{m,\alpha}(\beta)\leq F^*\}.} \tag{69}
\]

Joint continuity in (58) makes \(\mathcal J\) continuous.  Combining this
with Theorem 7 gives

\[
 \mathcal J(\beta_c)=F^*,\qquad
 \mathcal J(\beta)\leq F^*\ (\beta<\beta_c),
 \qquad
 \mathcal J(\beta)>F^*\ (\beta>\beta_c).        \tag{70}
\]

Thus (67)--(70) are an independently checkable one-dimensional
necessary-and-sufficient characterization of the full patience region.  They
do not assume monotonicity of an arbitrarily selected local equilibrium branch,
nor do they assume global single-peakedness of \(J_\beta\) in its cutoff
argument.

For completeness, when \(\alpha=0\) the strict-gain set is empty for every
\(m\): no incumbent reaches an accepting second-period state, so
\(D_{0,0}^*(m;\beta)=F_0^*(m)\) for all \(\beta\).  One may encode this
degenerate case by the convention \(\beta_c(m,0)=1\), but it is outside the
maintained \(\alpha>0\) patience-region theorem.
