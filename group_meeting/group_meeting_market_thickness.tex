\noindent\textbf{Preview.}
A publicly known single driver gives an exact cutoff-WPBE and a closed-form
optimal menu.  With latent Poisson supply, the completion value of rescue
pricing is small in thin markets, largest at an intermediate thickness, and
small again in thick markets.  For
$(\alpha,\beta,\delta,\gamma)=(1,.8,.8,0)$, the deterministic numerical
solution peaks near $m=9.72$ and improves optimized completion by $6.16$
percentage points.  The endpoint pattern is proved; strict single-peakedness
is a conjecture.

\section{Exact one-rider/one-driver benchmark}

\subsection{Closed-form solution}

The rider has $v\sim U[0,1]$, the driver has $c\sim U[0,1]$, and the platform
maximizes completion.  Delayed rider value is $\beta v$ and delayed incumbent
surplus is $\delta(p_j-c)$.  The platform commits to
$0\le p_1\le p_2\le1$.  The number of drivers is publicly known to be exactly
one; there is no survival shock or fresh entry.

For an active menu $0\le p_1<p_2<\beta$, the driver accepts immediately iff
$c\le\ch$.  After rejection, the rider cancels, repeats $p_1$, or activates
$p_2$ according to
\[
v<\frac{p_1}{\beta},\qquad
\frac{p_1}{\beta}\le v\le v^M(\ch),\qquad
v>v^M(\ch),
\qquad
v^M(\ch)=\frac{p_1+p_2-\ch}{\beta}.
\]
The marginal driver's accept-versus-wait indifference gives the WPBE cutoff
\begin{equation}
\ch(p_1,p_2)=\left[p_1-
\frac{\delta(p_2-p_1)(\beta-p_2)}
{\beta(1-p_1)-\delta(\beta-p_2)}\right]_+ ,
\tag{1}
\end{equation}
and equilibrium completion is
\begin{equation}
M_1(p_1,p_2)=(1-p_1)\ch+
\frac{(\beta-p_2)(p_2-\ch)}{\beta}.
\tag{2}
\end{equation}
Thus the cutoff is calculated from the WPBE; the platform then optimizes over
menus subject to this equilibrium response.

Fixing a target cutoff reduces the two-price problem to one dimension.  The
unique continuation envelope and its implementing first price are
\begin{equation}
\widehat p_2(\ch)=\frac{\beta+\ch}{2},\qquad
\widehat p_1(\ch)=
\frac{1+\ch-\sqrt{\Delta(\ch)}}2,\qquad
\Delta(\ch)=(1-\ch)^2-\frac{\delta}{\beta}(\beta-\ch)^2.
\tag{3}
\end{equation}
Substitution into (2) gives
\begin{equation}
M_1^*=\max\left\{\frac14,\ \max_{\ch\in[0,\beta]}J_\delta(\ch)\right\},
\quad
J_\delta(\ch)=
\frac{\ch[1-\ch+\sqrt{\Delta(\ch)}]}2
+\frac{(\beta-\ch)^2}{4\beta}.
\tag{4}
\end{equation}
If $\beta\le1/2$ or $\delta=1$, the optimal value is the flat benchmark
$1/4$.  If $\beta>1/2$ and $\delta<1$, $J_\delta$ is strictly concave and has
a unique interior maximizer, which implements $p_1^*<p_2^*$.

p_1^*=\frac{11+\sqrt{57}}{40}=.463746,\quad

\subsection{Equilibrium and the object reported later}

For continuous full-support $F$, every anonymous symmetric independent WPBE is
a.e. a pure cutoff: accept now iff $c\le a$.  The cutoff set
$\mathcal E_m(p_1,p_2)$ is nonempty and compact, so cutoff behavior is a result,
not an assumption.  Thus $a$, not $\alpha$, determines who waits.  Conditional
on choosing to defer, $\alpha$ only thins the screened incumbent pool.  After
failure, incumbents have $c>a$, entrants are unscreened, and
\[
\lambda_j(a)=m\!\left\{\alpha[F(p_j)-F(a)]+\gamma F(p_j)\right\},\quad
C_j(a)=1-e^{-\lambda_j(a)}.
\]
The rider compares $0$, $C_1(a)(\beta v-p_1)$, and
$C_2(a)(\beta v-p_2)$; driver indifference closes the equilibrium.  The
conservative comparison is
\[
\underline M_m(p_1,p_2)=\min_{a\in\mathcal E_m(p_1,p_2)}M_m(p_1,p_2;a),\quad
V(m)=\sup_{p_1\le p_2}\underline M_m(p_1,p_2)
      -\max_p\underline M_m(p,p).
\]
With $F=G=U[0,1]$ and $\gamma=0$, every menu has a unique cutoff.  Hence
$100V(m)$ is the percentage-point gain between optimized policies at fixed
$m$, not the marginal value of another driver.

\subsection{Two local propositions with $\alpha=1$}

Throughout this subsection, $\alpha=1$: an incumbent who chooses to defer is
certain to remain available after failure.  This does not make waiting
exogenous: costless deferral weakly dominates exit, but need not beat accepting
now.  The equilibrium cutoff separates drivers who accept now
($c\le a_\varepsilon$) from those who defer ($c>a_\varepsilon$).  Types with
$c>p+\varepsilon$ are indifferent between deferring and exiting at zero, a
tie that does not affect completion.

Let $h(z)=(1-e^{-z})/z$ with $h(0)=1$, and write
\[
x=mp,\quad R=e^{-x},\quad \sigma=h(x),\quad
\rho=\frac{1-p/\beta}{1-p}.
\]

\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1 (without fresh entry).}
Assume $F=G=U[0,1]$, $m>0$, $0<p<\beta<1$,
$\alpha=1$, $\gamma=0$, and $0<\delta\le1$.  For all small
$\varepsilon>0$, the menu
$(p,p+\varepsilon)$ has a unique anonymous symmetric
cutoff equilibrium and
\[
a_\varepsilon=p-\kappa_0(p)\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon),\qquad
\kappa_0(p)=\frac{\delta R\rho}{\sigma-\delta R\rho}>0,
\]
and
\[
M_m^{1,0}(p,p+\varepsilon;a_\varepsilon)
=M_0^{\rm flat}(m,p)+\Lambda_0(p)\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon),
\quad
\Lambda_0(p)=m(1-p)R\rho
\frac{\sigma-\delta R}{\sigma-\delta R\rho}>0. \tag{P1}
\]
Consequently, a small rescue strictly improves on any active flat price
$p<\beta$, including an optimized one.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Proof.}
Let $d=p-a$ and let $\eta_\varepsilon$ be the posting-conditional mass of
riders who activate rescue.  Without entry, terminal intensities are $md$ and
$m(d+\varepsilon)$, so cutoff indifference is
\[
h(ma)d=\delta e^{-ma}
\{(\rho-\eta_\varepsilon)h(md)d
+\eta_\varepsilon h(m(d+\varepsilon))(d+\varepsilon)\}. \tag{P2}
\]
Since $h(z)\le1$, $h(ma)\ge e^{-ma}$, and
$0\le\eta_\varepsilon\le\rho<1$, every equilibrium satisfies
\[
0\le d\le
\frac{\delta\rho}{1-\delta\rho}\varepsilon. \tag{P3}
\]
Hence $y=d/\varepsilon$ lies in a compact interval.  On that interval the
rider switch converges uniformly to $p/\beta$, so
$\eta_\varepsilon\to\rho$.  Dividing (P2) by $\varepsilon$ gives the limiting
equation
\[
(\sigma-\delta R\rho)y-\delta R\rho=0. \tag{P4}
\]
Its slope exceeds $\sigma-R>0$, yielding the unique local root and
$\kappa_0$ in (P1).  Direct differentiation of completion along this branch
gives $\Lambda_0$; it is positive because $\sigma>R\ge\delta R$ and the
denominator is positive. \hfill$\square$

\noindent\textbf{Proposition 2 (with fresh entry).}
Maintain the assumptions of Proposition 1 except let $\gamma>0$.  Define
\[
E=e^{-\gamma x},\qquad \ell=h(\gamma x),\qquad
\bar v=\frac1\beta\left[p+\frac{1-E}{mE(1+\gamma)}\right],\qquad
\eta^0=\frac{1-\bar v}{1-p},
\]
and the activity index
\[
\mathcal A_{1,\gamma}(p)
=mE(1+\gamma)(\beta-p)-(1-E).
\]
If $\mathcal A_{1,\gamma}(p)>0$, then for all small $\varepsilon>0$ the menu
$(p,p+\varepsilon)$ has a unique cutoff equilibrium and
\[
a_\varepsilon=p-\kappa_\gamma(p)\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon),\qquad
\kappa_\gamma(p)=
\frac{\delta R\ell\eta^0}{\sigma-\delta R\rho\ell}>0,
\]
and
\[
M_m^{1,\gamma}(p,p+\varepsilon;a_\varepsilon)
=M_\gamma^{\rm flat}(m,p)+\Lambda_\gamma(p)\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon),
\]
where
\[
\Lambda_\gamma(p)=m(1-p)R\eta^0
\frac{T_{\delta,\gamma}}{\sigma-\delta R\rho\ell}>0,
\quad
T_{\delta,\gamma}=E(1+\gamma)\sigma-
\delta R\ell\{1-\rho+\rho E(1+\gamma)\}. \tag{P5}
\]
If $\mathcal A_{1,\gamma}(p)<0$, then for all small $\varepsilon>0$ the
unique cutoff is $a=p$, no rider uses rescue, and completion is exactly
$M_\gamma^{\rm flat}(m,p)$.  No claim is made at the boundary
$\mathcal A_{1,\gamma}(p)=0$.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Proof.}
Again set $d=p-a$.  Fresh entry changes terminal intensities to
\[
\lambda_1=m(d+\gamma p),\qquad
\lambda_2=\lambda_1+m(1+\gamma)\varepsilon. \tag{P6}
\]
The cutoff equation is (P2) with $md$ and $m(d+\varepsilon)$ replaced by
$\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$; the same argument gives the localization (P3).
On $y=d/\varepsilon$, the rider switch now converges uniformly to $\bar v$,
and
\[
\mathcal A_{1,\gamma}(p)>0\iff\bar v<1.
\]
In the active case, $\eta_\varepsilon\to\eta^0>0$ and the limiting cutoff
equation is
\[
(\sigma-\delta R\rho\ell)y-\delta R\ell\eta^0=0. \tag{P7}
\]
Its slope exceeds $\sigma-R>0$, proving the cutoff expansion.  Differentiating
completion gives (P5); the activity inequality and exponential convexity imply
$T_{1,\gamma}>0$, while
\[
T_{\delta,\gamma}=T_{1,\gamma}+(1-\delta)R\ell
\{1-\rho+\rho E(1+\gamma)\}>0.
\]
In the inactive case, uniform convergence sets $\eta_\varepsilon=0$ on the
localized set, so only $d=0$ remains and rescue is unused. \hfill$\square$

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Connection and scope.}  At $\gamma=0$, $E=\ell=1$,
$\bar v=p/\beta$, $\eta^0=\rho$, and Proposition 2 reduces exactly to
Proposition 1.  Economically, without entry the screened pool leaves no
terminal supply at $p$, so a marginal rescue is automatically active.  With
entry, the flat payment already has coverage $1-E$, and rescue works only if
the activity test leaves riders willing to switch.  The propositions are
local around an active flat price; they do not solve the global mixed-entry
menu problem or assert monotonicity in $\gamma$.

\section{Literature position: the exact joint object}

\takeaway{Among the closest papers, none combines ex ante same-request rescue
commitment, latent competition, failure screening, and announcement-induced
current waiting.}

\noindent Each ingredient exists separately; the claim concerns their joint
equilibrium-design structure.

\subsection{How the literature disciplines this note}

Following these papers, the note solves equilibrium before design, traces the
reoptimized objective over the Poisson mean, then adds one composition
extension while keeping screened $\alpha$ supply separate from fresh $\gamma$
entry.  Commitment remains a theoretical channel, not an inference from
observed bonuses.

\noindent\textbf{Bottom line.}
The contribution is the joint cutoff-WPBE design under public failure.
Numerically, rescue matters at intermediate thickness; its gain is recovery
net of induced waiting; and continuation composition is not headcount.
